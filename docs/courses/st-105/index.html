Explore the biblical identity, purpose, government, ordinances, and mission of the Church as God's covenant community called to proclaim the Gospel and disciple the nations. Students study the Church's origin at Pentecost, its leadership structures, spiritual gifts, corporate worship, and the practice of baptism and communion. This course cultivates a healthy theology of community and belonging, preparing students to serve faithfully within a local church and understand their role in advancing God's Kingdom purposes.

The Church is not an organization you join. It is a people you become.
Dr. Kay Ijisesan, *What We Believe: The Church*, Chapter One

How This Course Is Built

The textbook moves in three parts, and this course follows them.

Part One establishes the Church's identity through four images: called-out people, Body, Bride, and household. Part Two describes her life together: worship, fellowship, prayer, the ordinances, and discipleship. Part Three turns to her mission: leadership, evangelism, the Kingdom, and her posture in the world.

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course the student will be able to

  1. Define the Church from ekklesia and distinguish it from the substitutes commonly mistaken for it.
  2. Explain the royal priesthood of all believers and its implications.
  3. Explain the Body metaphor and why unity is preserved through diversity rather than uniformity.
  4. Explain the Bride image, including how it holds security and faithfulness together.
  5. Explain the household image and the obligations of burden-bearing and regular gathering.
  6. Define worship in the fuller sense Romans 12:1 requires, and connect it to fellowship and generosity.
  7. Explain the structure of the Lord's Prayer and the place of corporate prayer.
  8. Explain both ordinances: what each signifies, what neither accomplishes, and why physical signs were given.
  9. Distinguish making converts from making disciples, and explain the four-generation chain of 2 Timothy 2:2.
  10. Explain the servant-shepherd model of leadership and the qualifications for elders and deacons.
  11. Explain the Church's evangelistic and missionary responsibility, including the ambassador framework.
  12. Distinguish the Church from the Kingdom without collapsing either into the other.
  13. Explain the salt and light images and the twin errors of withdrawal and assimilation.

Course Assignment

Write 900 to 1,200 words on the following.

Dr. Kay describes a particular kind of loneliness that afflicts even people who attend church regularly: present in a room full of believers and still profoundly alone. Using at least four passages from this course, explain what Scripture actually requires of church membership beyond attendance. Then assess one congregation you know well against that standard, naming specifically what would have to change.

You are graded on scriptural grounding, the honesty and specificity of the assessment, and whether your proposed changes are structural rather than merely aspirational.

Who Is the Holy Spirit?

A Person to know, fully God, not a force to be harnessed

Ask most Christians to describe God the Father, and they will reach for words fairly quickly. Ask them to describe Jesus, and the words come even faster, because we have His words, His footsteps, His face turned toward the people He touched. Ask the same believers to describe the Holy Spirit, and something changes. The sentences slow down. The language turns vague. He becomes a wind, a feeling, a mysterious force that shows up in worship or withdraws in silence.

That hesitation is understandable. It is not accurate, and it is not what Scripture gives us.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. State the identity of the Holy Spirit as the third Person of the Trinity, fully God and distinct in Person.
  2. Demonstrate His personhood from the range of personal actions Scripture attributes to Him.
  3. Explain the meaning of Helper and the three strokes of His ministry that Jesus described.
  4. Establish His deity from direct identification, from the attributes He possesses, and from the worship He is due.
  5. Explain why almost every confusion about the Spirit's ministry traces back to a faulty starting point.

Key Terms

ParacleteGreek parakletos, called alongside
The word Jesus used for the Spirit, translated Helper, Comforter, or Advocate. A legal and relational term for one called alongside to assist, defend, and strengthen.
Personhood
The possession of mind, will, and emotion. Scripture attributes all three to the Spirit, which no force possesses.
Deity
Full possession of the divine nature. The Spirit is as fully God as the Father and as fully God as the Son.
Procession
The theological term for the Spirit's eternal relation of origin within the Godhead, distinguishing His Person without dividing the divine essence.
Intercession of the Spirit
The Spirit's own activity of praying on the believer's behalf when the believer does not know how to pray as they ought (Romans 8:26 to 27).

1. Not a Force, But a Person

The Holy Spirit is not a force radiating from God. He is not an emotional atmosphere God creates when He is pleased with us. He is the third Person of the Trinity, as fully God as the Father and as fully God as the Son, distinct in Person yet undivided in essence.

Scripture is remarkably consistent in refusing to let us reduce Him to a thing.

He can be Which requires
Grieved (Ephesians 4:30) Emotions capable of being wounded
Lied to (Acts 5:3) An awareness that can be deceived
Resisted (Acts 7:51) A will that can be opposed
Heard speaking (Acts 13:2) Speech, and therefore mind
Interceding for us (Romans 8:26) A will engaged on our behalf

Each of these belongs to a Person, not a power.

If the Holy Spirit is merely an impersonal force, then our relationship to Him is mechanical. We learn the right techniques, we say the right words, we position ourselves correctly, and the force responds like electricity through a wire. But if He is a Person, as Scripture insists He is, then our relationship to Him is relational. We do not manipulate Him. We know Him. We do not extract power from Him. We walk with Him.
Chapter One
Two postures that both miss Him

Some believers treat the Holy Spirit almost like a vending machine, pressing the right spiritual buttons in hopes the desired outcome will drop into their hands. Others treat Him like a stranger, acknowledged in doctrine but never actually addressed, prayed to, or thanked. He wants neither to be used nor to be ignored. He wants to be known.

2. A Promise Before He Was a Presence

Long before the Spirit filled a room in Jerusalem, He was a promise on the lips of the prophets. Joel saw a day coming when God would pour out His Spirit on all flesh, when sons and daughters would prophesy, when old men would dream dreams and young men would see visions. He was not describing a private religious experience reserved for priests or kings. He was describing a flood that would reach everyone who called on the name of the Lord, regardless of age, gender, or status.

That promise sat unfulfilled for centuries. Then Jesus, on the night before His crucifixion, picked it back up and made it personal.

He was never meant to be a consolation prize for the ascended Christ. He is the means by which the ascended Christ becomes present to every believer, everywhere, all at once, forever. Jesus could only be in one place at a time during His earthly ministry. Through the Spirit, He is with His people always, to the end of the age.

3. The Word Jesus Chose

Jesus called Him the Helper, sometimes translated Comforter, sometimes Advocate. The underlying Greek word pictures someone called alongside another person to assist, defend, and strengthen them. It is a legal and relational term, the kind of word used for a friend who stands with you in a courtroom or a companion who walks beside you through hardship.

He described this Helper's ministry in three overlapping strokes.

  1. The Spirit would teach them all things and bring to their remembrance everything Jesus had said.
  2. The Spirit would testify about Jesus, keeping the focus of the Christian life fixed where it belongs.
  3. The Spirit would guide them into all truth, not inventing new revelation but unfolding the full implications of what Christ had already accomplished.

Every one of those functions requires a mind, a will, and a personality. Forces do not teach. Forces do not testify. Forces do not guide. Persons do.

4. A Helper for the Long Road

Jesus did not promise a Helper only for dramatic seasons, the mountaintop experiences of ministry success or spiritual breakthrough. He promised a Helper for the ordinary, grinding reality of daily discipleship, the seasons of confusion, delay, opposition, and fatigue that make up most of the Christian life.

The disciples who received this promise would go on to face imprisonment, hunger, shipwreck, betrayal, and, in most cases, violent death. The Helper Jesus promised was not a guarantee of ease. He was a guarantee of presence.

He is as fully present in a mundane Tuesday afternoon as He was in the upper room on the day of Pentecost. Learning to recognize Him there, in the unremarkable middle of your week, is often where the deepest, most durable friendship with Him actually grows.
Chapter One

5. Lying to God

Perhaps nowhere is the Spirit's deity stated more directly than in the account of Ananias and Sapphira. The couple sold a piece of property and brought a portion of the proceeds to the apostles, claiming it was the whole amount. Peter asked why Satan had filled Ananias's heart to lie to the Holy Spirit, and then, in the very next breath, told him he had not lied to men but to God.

Peter used the two terms, Holy Spirit and God, as perfect synonyms. There is no daylight between them in his accusation.

If Peter, writing Scripture, equated the two so casually, it is because the equation required no defense. Everyone in the early church already understood the Spirit to be divine.
Chapter Two

6. The Attributes Only God Possesses

Beyond direct identification, Scripture credits the Spirit with attributes belonging to God alone.

  • Eternal. Present at creation, hovering over the formless deep before time as we know it began.
  • Omniscient. Searching even the deep things of God, for the Spirit knows the thoughts of God the way your own spirit knows your own thoughts, an analogy Paul uses precisely because no creature could claim that kind of access to the mind of God.
  • Omnipresent. David asked where he could flee from God's Spirit and answered his own question by realising there was nowhere in heaven, in the depths, or across the farthest sea where the Spirit was not already present.
  • Omnipotent. Active in creation, in resurrection, and in the miracles that accompanied the ministry of Christ and the apostles.

No angel possesses these qualities. No created being holds omniscience, omnipresence, and omnipotence simultaneously. These are not borrowed attributes on loan from God. They belong to the Spirit because the Spirit is God.

7. Mind, Will, and Emotion

Deity establishes what the Spirit is. Personality establishes who He is, and the two work together rather than in competition.

Paul writes that the Spirit searches all things and knows the thoughts of God, language that requires intellect. He writes that the Spirit distributes gifts to each person individually as He wills, language that requires a will capable of decision. And he pleads with believers not to grieve the Holy Spirit of God, language that requires emotion.

You cannot grieve a force. You cannot wound electricity. Grief belongs to relationship, and relationship belongs to persons.
Chapter Two

The Spirit's intercession deserves special attention. When we do not know how to pray as we ought, the Spirit Himself intercedes for us with groanings that words cannot express (Romans 8:26 to 27). This is not a poetic way of describing our own inarticulate longing. It describes the Spirit's own activity on our behalf.

On your worst day, when prayer feels impossible and your heart is too tangled to explain itself to God, the Spirit is already praying for you, and praying rightly, because He knows exactly what you need even when you do not.

8. The Spirit Who Reveals Rather Than Conceals

Paul draws a comparison meant to humble every one of us. Who among men knows the thoughts of a man except the spirit of that man which is within him? In the same way, no one knows the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God (1 Corinthians 2:10 to 11).

The comparison is precise. Just as your own inner self has full access to your own thoughts in a way no outsider ever could, the Spirit has full access to the thoughts of God in a way no created being ever could.

Then Paul makes the turn that changes everything. We have received not the spirit of the world but the Spirit who is from God, so that we might understand the things freely given to us by God. The same Spirit who possesses full access to the mind of God has been given to us, not to keep us at a distance from divine truth but to bring us inside it.

9. Never Building a Following Around Himself

None of the three Persons operates independently of the others. The Father sends the Son. The Son sends the Spirit. The Spirit glorifies the Son and leads us to the Father. It is a relationship of perfect harmony, never rivalry, never competition for attention.

A test for any teaching about the Spirit

He is not interested in building a following around Himself. He is interested in bringing you home to the Father through the Son. Any teaching about the Spirit that centres attention on the Spirit rather than on Christ has drifted from the very pattern the Spirit Himself follows.

10. Why This Comes First

Almost every confusion about the Spirit's ministry traces back to a faulty starting point.

If you think of Him as a force, you will chase sensations and call them Him. If you think of Him as a lesser being subordinate to the Father and Son, you will hesitate to trust Him with the weight Scripture places on His shoulders. If you receive Him as Scripture presents Him, fully God, fully personal, sent by the Father and the Son to make His home in you, then everything that follows in this course will make sense.

Dr. Kay observes that believers who grow comfortable acknowledging the Spirit's full deity tend to grow far more sensitive to His presence in ordinary life. They stop waiting for dramatic moments and start recognising His voice in the quiet nudge toward kindness, the unexpected conviction over a careless word, the peace that steadies a fearful heart before a hard conversation.

Discussion Questions

  1. Dr. Kay describes two failing postures toward the Spirit: treating Him as a vending machine and treating Him as a stranger. Which is more common in your context, and what does each cost a believer?
  2. Jesus said it was to the disciples' advantage that He go away. Explain why, without diminishing the value of His earthly presence.
  3. Work through the table in section 1. Which of those personal actions had you never previously considered as evidence of personhood?
  4. Romans 8:26 to 27 says the Spirit prays for us when we cannot pray. How would you use this with someone who has stopped praying because they feel they are doing it badly?
  5. The Spirit does not build a following around Himself. Apply that test to teaching about the Spirit you have encountered. What passes, and what does not?

Reflection

Stop asking what the Spirit can do for you before you have asked who He is. This week, simply acknowledge His presence, quietly and honestly, the way you might acknowledge a friend who just walked into the room. Then, in one prayer, address Him directly by name and thank Him for interceding for you when your own words fail.

Summary

The Holy Spirit is not a force, an atmosphere, or a lesser being. He is the third Person of the Trinity, fully God, distinct in Person and undivided in essence. Scripture refuses to reduce Him to a thing: He can be grieved, lied to, resisted, and heard speaking, and He intercedes for believers who cannot form the words themselves. Jesus called Him the Helper, one called alongside to assist, defend, and strengthen, promised not only for dramatic seasons but for the long, ordinary road of discipleship. His deity is established directly, when Peter equates lying to the Spirit with lying to God, and through the attributes only God possesses. He possesses mind, will, and emotion, and He holds full access to the thoughts of God, which He gives us in order to bring us inside divine truth rather than keep us at a distance. And He never draws attention to Himself. Lesson 2 traces His work through Scripture's history.

Regeneration and the New Birth

The miracle only the Spirit can perform, and the direction it sets

A man came to Jesus at night, carrying with him decades of religious achievement. Nicodemus was a Pharisee, a ruler of the Jews, a teacher respected enough that Jesus later addressed him as the teacher of Israel. By every external measure available in his culture, Nicodemus had arrived. And yet something in him remained unsettled enough to send him searching in the darkness for a conversation he could not have in daylight.

Jesus did not ease him into it. Unless one is born again, He told Nicodemus, he cannot see the kingdom of God.

This conversation sits at the foundation of everything Scripture teaches about salvation, and it centres entirely on the work of the Holy Spirit.

By the end of this topic you will be able to

  1. Explain the new birth from John 3 and why religious credentials cannot produce it.
  2. Explain the significance of Jesus' wind imagery for evangelism and for the sovereignty of the Spirit.
  3. Explain why regeneration is a new creation rather than a renovation, using Paul's and Peter's language.
  4. State the human condition before regeneration from Ephesians 2 and why it rules out self-improvement.
  5. Relate faith to regeneration without collapsing either into the other.
  6. Explain how regeneration sets the direction of the entire Christian life.

Key Terms

RegenerationLatin re + generare, to be born again
The Spirit's specific work of imparting new spiritual life to a spiritually dead person.
New BirthGreek anothen, again or from above
Jesus' own image for regeneration in John 3, describing an origin outside the person entirely.
New Creation
Paul's language in 2 Corinthians 5:17, describing not an improvement of the old but the bringing into being of something genuinely new.
Incorruptible Seed
Peter's image in 1 Peter 1:23 for the permanence of the new birth, grounded in the eternal word of God rather than in the believer's consistency.
Monergism of Regeneration
The truth that the new birth is accomplished by God alone. Faith receives it; faith does not produce it.

1. Credentials Cannot Produce Life

Nicodemus, understandably confused, asked how a grown man could enter his mother's womb a second time. Jesus clarified that He was describing not a second physical birth but a spiritual one.

Nicodemus's religious credentials, however impressive, could not produce what only the Spirit could produce.

No amount of law-keeping, ritual observance, or moral effort generates spiritual life, because spiritual life is not manufactured. It is given, and it is given by the Spirit alone.
Chapter Five

2. The Wind That Cannot Be Controlled

Jesus reached for an image Nicodemus would have understood immediately. The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. The Greek word for wind and the word for spirit are the same word, and Jesus used that overlap deliberately.

Just as no one can control, predict, or fully explain the movement of the wind, no one can control, predict, or fully explain the moment the Spirit brings a person from spiritual death to spiritual life.

What this does and does not mean for evangelism

This should humble every believer tempted to reduce evangelism to a formula, as though the right sequence of words guarantees the right outcome every time. It is not, however, an excuse for passivity. Scripture calls us to proclaim the gospel with urgency and clarity. It is a reminder that conversion is never something we accomplish through technique. It is something the Spirit accomplishes through grace.

3. A New Creation, Not a Renovation

The language Paul uses is far more radical than most of us initially assume. If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. Old things have passed away. Behold, all things have become new (2 Corinthians 5:17).

Paul does not describe an improvement, an upgrade, or a renovation of the existing structure. He describes a new creation entirely, the same kind of language Genesis uses to describe God speaking the universe into existence out of nothing.

This corrects a common misunderstanding of conversion. Salvation is not God taking the old, sinful version of you and gradually sanding down the rough edges until something acceptable emerges. It is God creating something genuinely new where spiritual death previously existed.

Paul makes it explicit to Titus: salvation comes not because of works done in righteousness, but according to God's mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5).

4. Incorruptible Seed

Peter frames the same reality in terms of birth rather than creation. Believers have been born again, not of corruptible seed but incorruptible, through the word of God which lives and abides forever (1 Peter 1:23).

The permanence Peter describes matters enormously. A corruptible birth can decay, weaken, or be undone. An incorruptible birth cannot.

The permanence of your new birth does not rest on the consistency of your subsequent obedience. It rests on the nature of the seed that produced it, and that seed, God's own eternal word, cannot decay or fail.
Chapter Five

Natural birth, once it happens, cannot be reversed. You cannot become unborn. Peter applies that same permanence to spiritual birth. This should bring genuine relief to any believer haunted by fear that a season of struggle or doubt has cost them their standing before God.

5. Dead Men Do Not Raise Themselves

Paul's letter to the Ephesians paints the human condition before regeneration in language that leaves no room for spiritual self-improvement as a path to God. Believers were once dead in trespasses and sins, following the course of this world, by nature children of wrath.

Dead people do not respond to encouragement. Dead people do not improve their circumstances through effort. Dead people require someone else to intervene, and that is precisely Paul's point. But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ (Ephesians 2:4 to 5).

This is the miracle regeneration actually is. Not a nudge toward a better life, but resurrection from spiritual death.
Chapter Five

The same power that raised Jesus from the tomb is the power that raises a spiritually dead person into new life, and that power belongs to the Holy Spirit. This is why no one should ever feel they must clean themselves up before coming to Christ. The entire premise of regeneration is that we cannot.

6. Faith and the New Birth

A natural question follows. If regeneration is entirely the Spirit's work, what role does faith play?

Scripture holds both truths together without contradiction. We are saved by grace through faith, and this is not of ourselves, it is the gift of God (Ephesians 2:8 to 9). Faith is the means through which we receive the salvation the Spirit accomplishes, not a work that earns or produces it.

The Spirit opens spiritually blind eyes to see the truth and beauty of the gospel, and in response, a person believes. The believing is genuinely theirs. The capacity to believe rightly, in a way that actually receives Christ, is the Spirit's gift.

For the anxious believer

This should relieve any anxiety about whether your faith was strong enough, sincere enough, or informed enough to count. Salvation was never a transaction where your faith had to reach some minimum threshold of impressiveness before God would respond. The Spirit regenerates, and faith, however small, however trembling, however mixed with doubt, is the hand that receives what He has already given.

7. Living From the New Birth, Not Toward It

One of the most practical implications of regeneration is the direction it establishes for the entire Christian life.

Many believers, even years into their faith, unconsciously live as though they are working toward acceptance with God rather than living from an acceptance already secured. Regeneration means the decisive change has already happened.

You are not becoming a child of God through faithful effort. You already are one, born of the Spirit, a new creation, and everything that follows in the Christian life, every act of obedience, every step of growth, flows from that identity rather than toward it.
Chapter Five

This changes the emotional tone of the entire Christian walk. Obedience becomes the grateful response of someone who has already been given life, not the anxious striving of someone hoping to qualify for it. You stop approaching God as a probationary employee hoping for a good performance review and start approaching Him as a beloved child.

8. When Conversion Felt Ordinary

Some believers doubt whether their conversion was genuine because it lacked the drama they expected.

Remember Nicodemus. He came quietly, at night, uncertain, and Jesus did not require a particular emotional display before acknowledging the reality of the new birth available to him. What matters is not the intensity of the moment but the reality of the transformation, a transformation the Spirit alone accomplishes.

Summary

Nicodemus arrived with every credential his culture could confer and was told he must be born again. Religious effort cannot manufacture spiritual life, because life is given rather than produced. Jesus compared the new birth to wind, which humbles our confidence in technique without excusing passivity in proclamation. Paul calls the result a new creation, not a renovation, and Titus attributes it to the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Spirit. Peter grounds its permanence in incorruptible seed, so that the new birth cannot be undone by a bad season any more than a natural birth can be reversed. Ephesians establishes why: we were dead, and dead people need life given to them. Faith is the hand that receives, not the work that earns. And the whole thing sets a direction for the Christian life, which is lived from an acceptance already secured rather than toward one still in doubt. Topic 4 asks what the Spirit does once He has given that life.

Examine the person, deity, ministry, and work of the Holy Spirit in the life of the believer and the Church throughout redemptive history. Students study His role in conviction, regeneration, indwelling, sanctification, and empowerment for ministry, along with the gifts and fruit He produces in a surrendered life. Learn practical, biblical patterns for living under the Spirit's guidance and walking in His power daily, growing in intimacy with God and effectiveness in Kingdom service.

The Holy Spirit is not a doctrine to manage or an experience to chase. He is a Person to know.
Dr. Kay Ijisesan, *What We Believe: The Holy Spirit*, Preface

How This Course Is Built

The textbook is arranged in three movements, and this course follows them.

Part One introduces who the Holy Spirit is, tracing His identity and work from eternity past through the Old Testament and into the New Covenant He now administers. Part Two turns to what He does in the life of a believer, from the miracle of the new birth to the fruit He grows over time. Part Three looks outward, at the gifts He distributes, the daily walk He empowers, and the Church He continues to build.

The hinge of the whole course

Something changed at Pentecost. What had been occasional and selective in the Old Testament became permanent and universal in the New. The Spirit who once came upon a chosen few now comes to live within every believer. He does not visit us. He indwells us. If you miss that difference, you will spend your whole Christian life waiting for something you have already been given.

A Note on Perspective

Dr. Kay writes from a New Covenant perspective, which means he lets the finished work of Christ set the terms for how the Spirit's ministry is understood. The Spirit is not given to make up for something lacking in the cross. He is given because of what the cross accomplished. He does not come to make us worthy. He comes because we already are, in Christ.

The textbook also declines to referee every debate that has divided sincere believers over the Spirit. It addresses the ones Scripture speaks to clearly and leaves the rest. Instructors should hold the same line.

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course the student will be able to

  1. Demonstrate the personhood of the Holy Spirit from the range of personal actions Scripture attributes to Him.
  2. Establish His full deity from direct identification, divine attributes, and the worship due Him.
  3. Trace the Spirit's work through the Old Testament and state precisely what changed at Pentecost, and what did not.
  4. Explain regeneration as the sovereign work of the Spirit, and relate it correctly to faith.
  5. Explain the indwelling, the sealing, and the guarantee, and their bearing on assurance.
  6. Distinguish the baptism of the Spirit from the ongoing fullness of the Spirit, and explain how each is received.
  7. Distinguish fruit from gifts, and explain why gifts without love amount to nothing.
  8. Explain what it means to walk by the Spirit, including grieving and quenching Him.
  9. Describe the Spirit's active leadership of the Church in formation, mission, unity, and the equipping of leaders.

Course Assignment

Write 900 to 1,200 words on the following.

Dr. Kay writes that if you miss what changed at Pentecost, you will spend your whole Christian life waiting for something you have already been given. Explain what the Old Testament pattern of the Spirit's activity actually was, what changed under the New Covenant, and what did not change. Then describe two specific ways a believer's daily experience goes wrong when Old Testament patterns are imported into New Covenant life.

You are graded on accuracy of the historical trace, precision about the continuity and discontinuity, and the concreteness of the pastoral application.

Instructor Notes

This is the course where students are most likely to arrive with strong prior convictions formed by their tradition rather than by the text. Chapter 7 on baptism and fullness is the pressure point. Dr. Kay's distinction between one baptism and many fillings resolves most of the confusion, and it is worth teaching slowly and without polemic.

The other place to slow down is Chapter 3, on Saul losing the Spirit and David's plea in Psalm 51. Students need to feel the weight of the Old Covenant terms before the New Covenant contrast will land. Do not rush to the reassurance.

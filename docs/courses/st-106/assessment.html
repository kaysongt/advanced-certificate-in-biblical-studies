ST 106 · Final

Course Assessment

Eschatology: Our Blessed Hope

This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 106 and the whole of What We Believe, Volume 6: Eternity. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: 80%.

Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

Section A · 40 points

1What does the word angel establish about angelic beings?

Hebrews says they are ministering spirits sent forth to minister for those who will inherit salvation.

2What happens whenever a human being attempts to worship an angel in Scripture?

No matter how impressive a created thing appears, worship belongs to the Creator alone.

3According to the passages the textbook treats, what was the seed of Satan's fall?

A created being, given extraordinary glory and position, chose to want more.

4What does the structure of Job 1 establish about the adversary?

There is no contest of strength here, only a contest of time, and even that clock is running down.

5What does James 2:19 warn against?

The demons have perfect theology and no salvation whatsoever, because theirs is knowledge without trust, without love, without surrender.

6What single question separates biblical spiritual warfare from its counterfeits?

Ephesians describes everything believers already possess before it ever turns to warfare, and even then commands them to stand.

7What do all six pieces of the armour of God have in common?

Spiritual warfare in its most basic biblical form is the discipline of remembering and standing on what is already true.

8What order does James 4:7 establish?

A believer who resists in his own strength, without first submitting fully to God, is fighting with no foundation underneath him.

9Why does Scripture call death an enemy?

Christianity does not romanticize death. It confronts it honestly as the intrusion it always was.

10What does Scripture teach about the intermediate state?

Jesus told the thief today, and Paul called departing to be with Christ far better.

11What evidence tells against soul sleep?

These are not unconscious beings. They are aware, waiting, and able to speak.

12What does Acts 1:11 promise about the manner of Christ's return?

Not a metaphorical presence, but the same Jesus, exactly as He left.

13What do parousia and apokalypsis each contribute?

The One who came the first time in humility returns in the fullness of glory that has always belonged to Him.

14How does Peter answer those who mock the delay of Christ's return?

He is not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance.

15What does firstfruits establish about Christ's resurrection?

Everyone united to Him by faith will follow Him through the same door He opened.

16What does Paul mean by a spiritual body?

The risen Jesus ate broiled fish and invited Thomas to touch His wounds.

17What is evaluated at the judgment seat of Christ?

The bema was the raised platform where a judge awarded prizes to victorious competitors.

18What is the decisive factor at the great white throne?

Measured by our own works, none of us stand any chance of a favourable verdict.

19What phrase does the textbook identify as the theological heart of hell?

Every good thing exists only because God allows His goodness to reach even a rebellious creation. Hell is the complete withdrawal of that goodness.

20What is the difference between all things new and all new things?

Salvation from beginning to end is a story of restoration rather than replacement.

Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

B1. Explain why no true angel in Scripture accepts worship, and connect that refusal to the warning Paul gives in Colossians 2:18. Then state the two opposite errors believers make regarding the demonic and the course Scripture charts between them.

B2. Explain the difference between fighting for victory and fighting from it, using the structure of Ephesians. Then explain what Ephesians 6:12 does not license a believer to conclude about difficult people.

B3. Define the intermediate state and give two lines of evidence against soul sleep. Then explain why the textbook calls that state a waystation rather than the destination.

B4. Distinguish the judgment seat of Christ from the great white throne: who appears, what is at issue, and what is at stake. Then explain how works can function at judgment if salvation is by faith alone.

B5. Explain why the textbook insists the new creation is renewal rather than replacement, using Revelation 21:5 and one other passage. Then state two practical implications for how a believer treats present labour or the physical world.

Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

Dr. Kay writes that any presentation of hell that seems eager or comfortable has almost certainly misunderstood something essential, and that the doctrine rightly held does not make a person harsher but makes the gospel sound louder. Using at least four passages from this course, explain why hell exists in terms of God's character rather than divine vindictiveness, and give an honest answer to the objection that eternal punishment does not fit a finite lifetime of sin. Then describe how you would actually teach this doctrine to a congregation that includes someone recently bereaved of an unbelieving parent.

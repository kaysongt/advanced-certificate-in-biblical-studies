Discover the person and work of Jesus Christ as the eternal Son of God, the promised Messiah, and the only Savior of humanity. This course traces His incarnation, sinless life, substitutionary death, bodily resurrection, and glorious ascension, revealing Him as the fulfillment of every Old Testament promise and shadow. Students gain a deep, worshipful understanding of Christ's divine and human natures and His ongoing reign, equipped to proclaim Jesus as the foundation and center of the Christian faith.

People often know facts about Jesus long before they know Him. They can recite the Christmas story and the Easter story. They can sing the songs. But ask them to explain why His humanity mattered, or what His current ministry in heaven looks like, or why the doctrine of His full deity is not a technicality but a matter of eternal life and death, and the room often goes quiet.
Dr. Kay Ijisesan, *What We Believe: Jesus Christ*, Preface

How This Course Is Built

The textbook moves in three parts, tracing Christ from before time began to His promised return. This course keeps that structure.

Part One, the Person of Christ, asks who He is. Part Two, the Work of Christ, asks what He has done. Part Three, the Exalted Christ, asks what He is doing now and what He will do.

The order matters for the same reason it mattered in ST 101. A student who begins at the cross without first establishing who was on it will not be able to say why the cross saves anyone.

Why this doctrine is handled carefully

Throughout church history the doctrine of Christ has been refined through long seasons of careful debate, controversy, and at times painful division. Councils gathered and language was sharpened, not because the early church enjoyed argument, but because getting this particular doctrine wrong carries eternal consequences. A wrong view of Jesus produces a wrong gospel, and a wrong gospel cannot save anyone.

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course the student will be able to

  1. Demonstrate from Scripture that the Son existed eternally before Bethlehem, and explain why His preexistence is necessary for salvation.
  2. Explain the incarnation, including the virgin birth and the kenosis, and state precisely what Christ did and did not set aside.
  3. Defend both the full humanity and the full deity of Christ, and state the doctrine of the two natures within its orthodox boundaries.
  4. Explain Christ's threefold office as Prophet, Priest, and King, and show what would be missing if He held only one or two.
  5. Describe the earthly ministry of Jesus as a deliberate unveiling of the Kingdom rather than a collection of good deeds.
  6. Explain what the cross accomplished under the headings of atonement, reconciliation, justification, the great exchange, and victory.
  7. Present the historical case for the resurrection and state the four things it confirms.
  8. Explain the ascension, Christ's present ministry of intercession and advocacy, and the nature of His promised return.

Course Assignment

Write 900 to 1,200 words on the following.

A member of your congregation tells you they are comfortable calling Jesus a great teacher and a moral example, but they find the claim that He is God difficult and unnecessary. Write your response. Establish the deity of Christ from at least four passages drawn from more than one category of evidence, explain why the doctrine cannot be softened without collapsing the gospel, and do it in language a thoughtful person outside theological training could follow.

You are graded on accuracy, breadth of scriptural support, the strength of the argument connecting deity to salvation, and pastoral tone. Winning the argument while losing the person is not a pass.

Instructor Notes

Two chapters generate the most discussion. Chapter 3 unsettles students who have quietly assumed Jesus moved through His earthly life with divine immunity to ordinary difficulty. Let that discomfort sit before resolving it, because the comfort of Hebrews 4:15 only lands if the temptation was real.

Chapter 10 is the one most students have never been taught at all. Dr. Kay notes that Christ's present ministry is among the most overlooked truths in popular Christian teaching. Give it full weight in Lesson 6 rather than treating it as a bridge between the ascension and the return.

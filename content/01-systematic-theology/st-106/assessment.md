This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 106 and the whole of *What We Believe, Volume 6: Eternity*. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: **80%**.

## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q What does the word *angel* establish about angelic beings?
+ Messenger, establishing that they are sent by God rather than acting on an agenda of their own
- Guardian, establishing a permanent assignment to each believer
- Servant, establishing their subordination to redeemed humanity
- Spirit, establishing that they cannot take visible form
= Hebrews says they are ministering spirits sent forth to minister for those who will inherit salvation.

Q What happens whenever a human being attempts to worship an angel in Scripture?
+ The angel refuses, calls himself a fellow servant, and directs worship to God
- The angel accepts it as appropriate to his rank
- The response varies by angelic order
- No such attempt is recorded
= No matter how impressive a created thing appears, worship belongs to the Creator alone.

Q According to the passages the textbook treats, what was the seed of Satan's fall?
+ Pride, wanting God's own place rather than the place God had given him
- Envy of humanity's role in creation
- Deception by another fallen being
- Resentment of the limits placed on his authority
= A created being, given extraordinary glory and position, chose to want more.

Q What does the structure of Job 1 establish about the adversary?
+ He operates within limits set by God and must receive explicit permission
- He holds authority over the earth comparable to God's
- His power is exercised only against unbelievers
- The outcome of his opposition is genuinely uncertain
= There is no contest of strength here, only a contest of time, and even that clock is running down.

Q What does James 2:19 warn against?
+ Reducing Christianity to facts assented to in the mind, since the demons believe and shudder
- Debating theology with those outside the faith
- Teaching doctrine without accompanying works of charity
- Claiming knowledge of the demonic realm without experience
= The demons have perfect theology and no salvation whatsoever, because theirs is knowledge without trust, without love, without surrender.

Q What single question separates biblical spiritual warfare from its counterfeits?
+ Whether you are fighting for victory or from victory
- Whether the conflict is spiritual or circumstantial
- Whether warfare is corporate or individual
- Whether the believer has authority to confront demonic powers
= Ephesians describes everything believers already possess before it ever turns to warfare, and even then commands them to stand.

Q What do all six pieces of the armour of God have in common?
+ Each describes the gospel from a different angle, already possessed rather than manufactured
- Each corresponds to a daily spiritual discipline
- Each is granted at a distinct stage of maturity
- Each addresses a different category of demonic attack
= Spiritual warfare in its most basic biblical form is the discipline of remembering and standing on what is already true.

Q What order does James 4:7 establish?
+ Submission to God first, and resistance to the enemy following from it
- Resistance first, with submission as its fruit
- The two are simultaneous and cannot be ordered
- Resistance is required only after repeated submission
= A believer who resists in his own strength, without first submitting fully to God, is fighting with no foundation underneath him.

Q Why does Scripture call death an enemy?
+ Because it was not part of God's original design and entered through sin
- Because it separates believers from the community of faith
- Because its timing cannot be predicted
- Because it interrupts the work God has assigned
= Christianity does not romanticize death. It confronts it honestly as the intrusion it always was.

Q What does Scripture teach about the intermediate state?
+ The believer is immediately present with the Lord, conscious and aware
- The believer sleeps unconsciously until the resurrection
- The believer undergoes a period of purification
- Scripture is entirely silent on the question
= Jesus told the thief *today*, and Paul called departing to be with Christ far better.

Q What evidence tells against soul sleep?
+ The souls under the altar in Revelation 6 speak and are given robes, and Moses and Elijah converse at the Transfiguration
- The silence of the Old Testament on the subject
- Paul's exclusively figurative use of the word *sleep*
- The absence of the doctrine from the early creeds
= These are not unconscious beings. They are aware, waiting, and able to speak.

Q What does Acts 1:11 promise about the manner of Christ's return?
+ The same Jesus, coming in the same way He was seen to go, visibly and bodily
- A spiritual return through the growth of the Church
- A secret coming unseen by the world
- A manner Scripture deliberately leaves unspecified
= Not a metaphorical presence, but the same Jesus, exactly as He left.

Q What do *parousia* and *apokalypsis* each contribute?
+ Royal arrival, and public unveiling of a glory now hidden
- Sudden seizure, and final judgment
- Secret coming, and later public appearing
- Bodily resurrection, and cosmic renewal
= The One who came the first time in humility returns in the fullness of glory that has always belonged to Him.

Q How does Peter answer those who mock the delay of Christ's return?
+ The delay is patience, giving space for repentance, not forgetfulness
- The mockers have misread the prophetic timeline
- The promise applies to a future generation
- The delay tests the endurance of the church
= He is not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance.

Q What does *firstfruits* establish about Christ's resurrection?
+ It is the first installment and guarantee of a larger harvest
- It is the most significant of several resurrections in Scripture
- It fulfilled the offering law symbolically only
- It marks the beginning of the church age
= Everyone united to Him by faith will follow Him through the same door He opened.

Q What does Paul mean by a *spiritual body*?
+ A perfected physical body, no longer subject to decay and fully responsive to the Spirit
- An immaterial, ghostlike form
- The soul in its disembodied state
- A body unrelated to the present one
= The risen Jesus ate broiled fish and invited Thomas to touch His wounds.

Q What is evaluated at the judgment seat of Christ?
+ The quality of a believer's works after salvation, for the purpose of reward
- Whether the believer's faith was genuine
- The believer's doctrinal fidelity
- The eternal destiny of all people
= The *bema* was the raised platform where a judge awarded prizes to victorious competitors.

Q What is the decisive factor at the great white throne?
+ Whether a person's name is written in the book of life, which belongs to the Lamb who was slain
- The balance of good deeds against bad in the books
- Whether a person had access to the gospel
- The testimony of the angels
= Measured by our own works, none of us stand any chance of a favourable verdict.

Q What phrase does the textbook identify as the theological heart of hell?
+ Away from the presence of the Lord
- The worm that does not die
- Weeping and gnashing of teeth
- The lake of fire
= Every good thing exists only because God allows His goodness to reach even a rebellious creation. Hell is the complete withdrawal of that goodness.

Q What is the difference between *all things new* and *all new things*?
+ Renewal of what already exists rather than replacement with something unrelated
- A distinction in the timing of the new creation
- A distinction between spiritual and material renewal
- A translation variant with no theological weight
= Salvation from beginning to end is a story of restoration rather than replacement.
:::

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

**B1.** Explain why no true angel in Scripture accepts worship, and connect that refusal to the warning Paul gives in Colossians 2:18. Then state the two opposite errors believers make regarding the demonic and the course Scripture charts between them.

**B2.** Explain the difference between fighting *for* victory and fighting *from* it, using the structure of Ephesians. Then explain what Ephesians 6:12 does not license a believer to conclude about difficult people.

**B3.** Define the intermediate state and give two lines of evidence against soul sleep. Then explain why the textbook calls that state a waystation rather than the destination.

**B4.** Distinguish the judgment seat of Christ from the great white throne: who appears, what is at issue, and what is at stake. Then explain how works can function at judgment if salvation is by faith alone.

**B5.** Explain why the textbook insists the new creation is renewal rather than replacement, using Revelation 21:5 and one other passage. Then state two practical implications for how a believer treats present labour or the physical world.

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> Dr. Kay writes that any presentation of hell that seems eager or comfortable has almost certainly misunderstood something essential, and that the doctrine rightly held does not make a person harsher but makes the gospel sound louder. Using at least four passages from this course, explain why hell exists in terms of God's character rather than divine vindictiveness, and give an honest answer to the objection that eternal punishment does not fit a finite lifetime of sin. Then describe how you would actually teach this doctrine to a congregation that includes someone recently bereaved of an unbelieving parent.

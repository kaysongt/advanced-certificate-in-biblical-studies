Explore the biblical revelation of God: His nature, attributes, sovereignty, and works. Students examine His holiness, love, omniscience, omnipotence, and omnipresence as revealed throughout Scripture, along with His eternal purposes and unfailing covenant faithfulness toward His people. This course establishes a thoroughly God-centered worldview, teaching believers to approach every area of life, relationship, and ministry from a settled confidence in who God is, equipped to worship rightly, trust deeply, and represent the Creator's character with conviction.

:::quote Dr. Kay Ijisesan, *What We Believe: God*, Preface
Every conversation about the Christian faith has to start somewhere, and if we are honest about where the Bible itself begins, it does not start with us. It starts with God.
:::

## How This Course Is Built

The textbook moves in two parts, and this course follows that same order because the order itself is an argument. Before we can say anything responsible about who God is, we have to establish how we know anything about Him at all. Part One asks how God has spoken. Part Two asks who God is.

Lessons 1 through 3 cover Part One. Lessons 4 through 6 cover Part Two. A student who skips ahead to the attributes of God without working through revelation, Scripture, and interpretation will end up with conclusions they cannot defend and cannot trace back to a text.

## Course Learning Outcomes

:::objectives By the end of this course the student will be able to
1. Define theology and systematic theology, and explain why doctrine is the inheritance of every believer rather than the specialty of scholars.
2. Distinguish general revelation from special revelation, and explain the relationship between revelation, inspiration, and illumination.
3. State and defend the inspiration, authority, sufficiency, and inerrancy of Scripture, and give a reasoned answer to common objections about textual reliability and the canon.
4. Apply sound interpretive principles to a passage, including context, genre, the analogy of Scripture, covenantal setting, and the Christ-centered focus of the whole Bible.
5. Trace the storyline of redemption from creation to new creation, and locate any given passage within it.
6. Explain God's self-existence, eternality, sovereignty, holiness, uniqueness, and personal knowability, and show how each is grounded in specific texts.
7. Distinguish communicable from incommunicable attributes, and show how the attributes interpret and protect one another.
8. State the doctrine of the Trinity accurately, identify modalism and tritheism as departures from it, and explain why the doctrine shapes prayer, worship, and mission.
9. Explain the believer's adoption as a child of God and its bearing on assurance, prayer, discipline, and identity.
:::

## Course Assignment

Write 900 to 1,200 words on the following.

> Choose one attribute of God covered in this course. Show from at least four passages of Scripture how that attribute is revealed, explain how it relates to at least two other attributes, and describe one specific way a settled confidence in this attribute would change how a believer responds to disappointment.

You are graded on accuracy of exegesis, breadth of scriptural support, coherence of the theological connections you draw, and the honesty and concreteness of the application. Devotional warmth is welcome. Vagueness is not.

## Instructor Notes

The most common failure in a course like this is that students learn to recite the attributes without ever being moved by them. Dr. Kay makes the point repeatedly that theology which stops at accurate description has fallen short of its purpose. Build worship into the teaching rather than appending it at the end.

Two places consistently generate questions. The first is the relationship between God's sovereignty and human suffering, which surfaces in Topic 4 and again in Topic 5. The book does not resolve it with a formula, and neither should the instructor. The second is the Trinity in Topic 6, where students often feel guilty for not fully understanding it. Relieve that burden early, as the textbook does, before teaching the distinctions.

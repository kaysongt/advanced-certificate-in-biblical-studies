If theology is the study of God, the next question has to be this. How could finite, sinful human beings ever come to know an infinite and holy God in the first place? Left to ourselves, we could not. Human wisdom, however impressive, cannot climb its way up to heaven and discover the mind of God through unaided reason or spiritual intuition.

The prophet Isaiah records the Lord declaring that His thoughts are not our thoughts, and His ways are not our ways, and that as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are His ways higher than ours (Isaiah 55:8 to 9). The gap between us and God is not a gap that human effort can close. It can only be closed from God's side.

This lesson covers two chapters because they answer one question in two stages. Chapter 2 asks how God has spoken. Chapter 3 asks whether the record of that speech, the Bible we hold, can be trusted.

:::objectives By the end of this lesson you will be able to
1. Define revelation, and explain why it must originate from God's side rather than ours.
2. Distinguish general revelation from special revelation, state what each can and cannot accomplish, and support both from Scripture.
3. Distinguish revelation, inspiration, and illumination, and explain why all three are necessary.
4. Explain plenary verbal inspiration, including what it does not mean.
5. Explain how the canon was recognized rather than invented, and name the criteria used.
6. Define the authority, sufficiency, and inerrancy of Scripture, and answer common objections about textual reliability.
:::

## Key Scriptures

:::scripture Hebrews 1:1 to 2
God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us by His Son.
:::

Notice the movement in that sentence. For centuries God spoke through prophets, dreams, visions, the law at Sinai, the tabernacle ordinances, the poetry of the Psalms. Each was a genuine word from God, and each was partial, a piece of a larger picture still coming into focus. Then, in the fullness of time, God spoke in a way that surpassed everything before it.

- **Isaiah 55:8 to 9** — the gap between God's thoughts and ours
- **Psalm 19:1 to 4** — the heavens declare the glory of God
- **Romans 1:18 to 20** — creation leaves humanity without excuse; truth suppressed in unrighteousness
- **Hebrews 1:1 to 3** — progressive revelation culminating in the Son
- **John 1:1 to 3, 1:18** — the Word who declares the Father
- **1 Corinthians 2:9 to 16** — the Spirit enabling us to understand
- **Romans 10:14, 17** — faith comes by hearing
- **2 Timothy 3:16 to 17** — all Scripture is God-breathed
- **2 Peter 1:20 to 21** — holy men spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit
- **Isaiah 40:8** — the word of our God stands forever
- **Luke 24:27, 44 to 45** — Jesus treats the Hebrew Scriptures as authoritative and Christ-centered
- **John 17:17** — Your word is truth

## Key Terms

:::terms
Revelation | Latin *revelare*, to unveil | God's gracious act of making Himself known to those who could never have found Him on their own. The content God has disclosed.
General Revelation | | What God has made known about Himself through creation and the human conscience, available in some measure to every person who has ever lived.
Special Revelation | | God's self-disclosure through His spoken and written word, through His mighty acts in history, and ultimately and most fully through His Son.
Inspiration | Greek *theopneustos*, God-breathed | The process by which God ensured that the human authors of Scripture recorded revelation accurately, so that what they wrote is truly God's word.
Illumination | | The ongoing work of the Holy Spirit enabling believers to understand and apply what has been revealed and inspired.
Plenary Verbal Inspiration | *plenary*, full | The doctrine that all of Scripture is inspired, and that inspiration extends to the words chosen rather than only to the general ideas.
Canon | Greek *kanon*, rule or measuring standard | The collection of books the church recognized as carrying the marks of divine inspiration.
Sufficiency | | That Scripture contains everything necessary for salvation and for a life of faithful obedience, so no further revelation is required to supplement it.
Inerrancy | | That Scripture, in its original manuscripts, is entirely truthful and without error in everything it affirms, when properly interpreted according to its own intended meaning.
:::

## 1. General Revelation: What Creation Can Say

The psalmist writes that the heavens declare the glory of God, and the sky above proclaims the work of His hands, that day after day they pour out speech, and night after night they reveal knowledge, and that there is no speech or language where their voice is not heard (Psalm 19:1 to 4).

Paul makes a bolder claim still. What can be known about God is plain to people, because God has shown it to them, since His invisible attributes, namely His eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen since the creation of the world, being understood through what has been made, so that people are without excuse (Romans 1:19 to 20).

That is a striking statement. It means no person will ever stand before God and claim genuine ignorance of His existence.

:::quote Chapter Two
The problem humanity faces is not a lack of evidence. It is a willful suppression of the truth in unrighteousness, a refusal to honor the God whose fingerprints are everywhere.
:::

## 2. What General Revelation Cannot Do

Creation can tell us that God exists, that He is powerful, that He is orderly and wise. Creation alone cannot tell us how a sinful person can be made right with a holy God. Nature does not preach the gospel.

This is why Scripture insists on the necessity of hearing. Paul asks how people will call on someone they have not believed in, and how they will believe in someone they have not heard of, and how they will hear without someone preaching, concluding that faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God (Romans 10:14, 17).

General revelation exposes our need. Special revelation, proclaimed through the church's witness, meets that need with the specific good news of Jesus Christ. Missions is not optional in a world where general revelation is universal, precisely because general revelation was never able to save anyone.

## 3. Special Revelation and Its Progressive Shape

Jesus Christ is not simply one more prophet added to a long list of messengers. He is the final and definitive Word of God. John identifies Jesus as the Word, present with God in the beginning and Himself fully God, through whom all things were made (John 1:1 to 3), and later writes that no one has seen God at any time, but the only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him (John 1:18). Hebrews calls Him the exact representation of God's nature, the radiance of His glory (Hebrews 1:3).

A question follows naturally. If Christ is the definitive Word, why did God bother with centuries of earlier revelation? Why not send His Son from the beginning?

The answer the textbook gives involves both human readiness and divine wisdom. The law given through Moses revealed humanity's sinfulness with a clarity general revelation could never achieve, exposing our need for a Savior long before that Savior arrived. Paul makes the connection directly: the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith (Galatians 3:24).

:::quote Chapter Two
The centuries of prophecy, sacrifice, and covenant history were not wasted time before the real story began. They were the real story, unfolding exactly as God intended.
:::

When Christ finally came at what Paul calls the fullness of time (Galatians 4:4), He came into a world carefully prepared to recognize and receive Him.

## 4. Three Words That Are Often Confused

These three are frequently used interchangeably in conversation and should not be.

| Term | What it refers to |
|---|---|
| Revelation | The content God has made known. The truth itself. |
| Inspiration | The process by which God ensured the human authors recorded that revelation accurately. |
| Illumination | The Spirit's ongoing work enabling believers to understand and apply what has been revealed and inspired. |

Paul touches on the third when he tells the Corinthians that the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him, and he is not able to understand them, because they are discerned spiritually, but that we have received the Spirit who is from God, that we might understand the things freely given to us by God (1 Corinthians 2:9 to 16).

Revelation gives us the truth. Inspiration guarantees that truth was recorded reliably. Illumination opens our eyes to receive it. All three are necessary, and all three are gifts of grace.

## 5. The Direction of the Whole Story

:::quote Chapter Two
From His first question to Adam in the garden, where are you, to His final invitation in Revelation, come, the movement of Scripture is consistently God pursuing His creatures rather than His creatures successfully hunting Him down through cleverness.
:::

This shapes the posture of the entire discipline. We do not approach the study of God as detectives piecing together clues from a distance. We approach it as children listening to a Father who has chosen, out of love, to speak.

Revelation is also not confined to words. God has revealed Himself through His mighty acts in history, moments when His character and power broke into human affairs unmistakably. The exodus from Egypt became the defining reference point for Israel's understanding of God for the rest of the Old Testament. Centuries later, prophets still reminded the people that the God who delivered them from Pharaoh's hand was the same God calling them back to faithfulness.

## 6. Inspiration: What Scripture Claims About Itself

Paul tells Timothy that all Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, so that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work (2 Timothy 3:16 to 17). The word translated *inspiration* literally means God-breathed. Scripture did not originate in the creative imagination of ancient religious thinkers reaching upward toward the divine. It originated in the breath of God Himself, moving outward toward humanity.

Peter says something similar. Prophecy never came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit (2 Peter 1:20 to 21).

**Plenary** means full, indicating that all of Scripture is inspired, not merely the parts dealing with theology or morality. **Verbal** means inspiration extends to the words chosen, not merely to the general ideas.

:::note What this does not mean
The biblical authors were not passive typists receiving dictation. Luke's careful historical prose reads differently than John's simpler, more repetitive style, which reads differently again than the poetic intensity of Isaiah. God worked through each author's vocabulary, background, and personality, while still ensuring that what was written was exactly what He intended to communicate.
:::

## 7. Authority, Canon, and Sufficiency

**Authority.** Because Scripture is inspired by God, it carries His authority. If the Bible were merely a helpful collection of ancient wisdom, we would be free to weigh it against our preferences and discard whatever felt outdated. If it is truly God-breathed, it comes to us not as a suggestion but as the final word on any matter it addresses. Isaiah declares that the grass withers and the flower fades, but the word of our God stands forever (Isaiah 40:8).

**Canon.** The church did not invent the canon by voting books into existence at a council, as popular myths suggest. The church *recognized* which books already carried the marks of divine inspiration, testing them against three criteria:

1. Apostolic origin or connection
2. Consistency with previously recognized Scripture
3. Widespread acceptance and use by the church across different regions

The Old Testament books were already firmly established as Scripture in Jewish tradition well before the time of Christ, and Jesus Himself referred to them with full authority, at one point summarizing the entire Hebrew canon from Genesis to Chronicles when He spoke of the blood of all the prophets shed from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah (Luke 11:50 to 51).

**Sufficiency.** Scripture contains everything necessary for salvation and for faithful obedience, so we do not need additional revelation, secret knowledge, or new prophetic words to supplement what has been given.

:::note A precise boundary
Sufficiency does not mean the Bible answers every conceivable question, such as which career to choose or which house to buy. It means the Bible gives us everything we need to know God, to be saved, and to live in a way that pleases Him.
:::

## 8. Testing Experience by Scripture

Believers often ask whether personal experiences, dreams, or a strong inner sense of conviction should carry equal weight alongside Scripture. God can and does work through experience, through the promptings of the Spirit, through wise counsel and providential circumstances. None of these should ever be placed on the same level of authority as the written word, nor allowed to contradict it.

Any experience, however powerful it may feel, must be tested against Scripture, not the other way around. Jesus told His Father in prayer that His word is truth (John 17:17), and that statement leaves no room for competing authorities.

## 9. Two Honest Objections

**Textual reliability.** Skeptics point out that we do not possess the original manuscripts, and that copies were made by hand for centuries before the printing press. The concern deserves a serious answer rather than a dismissive one. The New Testament is supported by a manuscript tradition unmatched by virtually any other work of ancient literature, with thousands of Greek manuscripts, along with early translations and quotations from early church writers, allowing scholars to compare texts with remarkable precision. The small number of variations that do exist overwhelmingly involve minor matters of spelling or word order. No essential Christian doctrine hangs on a disputed textual variant.

**Inerrancy and modern precision.** Inerrancy means Scripture, in its original manuscripts, is entirely truthful in everything it affirms, when properly interpreted according to its own intended meaning. It does not mean every number must match modern statistical precision, since ancient Near Eastern writers often used numbers in stylized or rounded ways well understood by their original audience. It does not mean every quotation is reproduced with the word-for-word precision of a modern court reporter.

:::warn A common category error
Rightly understanding inerrancy requires understanding the literary conventions the biblical authors were working within, rather than imposing anachronistic expectations onto an ancient text and then accusing it of failing standards it was never trying to meet.
:::

## 10. The Unity of Scripture and Its Purpose

The Bible was written by roughly forty authors, across approximately fifteen centuries, spanning three continents, in multiple languages, by kings, shepherds, fishermen, physicians, tax collectors, and prophets, addressing subjects from cosmology to law to poetry to prophecy to personal correspondence. Read together, these sixty-six books tell one coherent story, with one consistent portrait of God's character, one unified moral vision, and one central redemptive plan culminating in Jesus Christ.

Human collections assembled across such a span of time and circumstance do not typically hold together this way without deliberate coordinated planning. The explanation Christians have offered across the centuries is the simplest one available. Behind the many human authors stands one divine author.

:::quote Chapter Three
Scripture exists to reveal Jesus Christ and to draw people into saving, transforming relationship with Him through the power of the Holy Spirit. Every page, rightly read, points somewhere, and that somewhere is a Person.
:::

## Summary

The gap between us and God can only be closed from His side, and revelation is His gracious act of closing it. General revelation, through creation and conscience, reaches every person and leaves everyone without excuse, but it cannot preach the gospel. Special revelation does, unfolding progressively through prophets and covenant history and arriving fully in the Son. The truth God revealed was recorded reliably through inspiration and is received through the Spirit's illumination. Because Scripture is God-breathed it carries God's authority, and the church recognized rather than invented the canon that preserves it. Scripture is sufficient for salvation and obedience, truthful in all it affirms when read on its own terms, and remarkably unified across forty authors and fifteen centuries. All of it exists to reveal one Person. Lesson 3 turns to reading it rightly.

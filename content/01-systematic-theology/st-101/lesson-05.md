Topic 4 asked who God is at the level of His essential nature. This lesson asks what God is like, meaning the specific characteristics Scripture uses to describe His character. Theologians call these His attributes.

Dr. Kay is careful about how they should be handled.

:::quote Chapter Seven
I want to walk through several of the most important ones together, not as an isolated checklist to memorize but as facets of one unified, harmonious character, each one perfectly consistent with all the others.
:::

That framing carries the weight of the whole lesson. Most distortions in Christian teaching come not from denying an attribute but from isolating one and letting it swallow the rest.

:::objectives By the end of this topic you will be able to
1. Distinguish communicable from incommunicable attributes and explain what the distinction is for.
2. Explain why holiness functions as the lens through which every other attribute is read.
3. Define God's love, justice, mercy, and grace from Scripture, and show how the cross expresses all four at once.
4. Define omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence, and immutability with their supporting texts.
5. Show how each attribute protects the others from being misunderstood, and diagnose the distortion that results when one is isolated.
6. Connect each attribute to a corresponding calling on the believer's character.
:::

## Key Terms

:::terms
Communicable Attributes | | Qualities God shares, in a limited and creaturely way, with human beings made in His image, such as love, holiness, wisdom, and faithfulness.
Incommunicable Attributes | | Qualities that belong to God alone and cannot be shared with creatures, such as omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence, and self-existence.
Omnipotence | Latin *omni* + *potens*, all-powerful | God possesses all power, able to accomplish anything consistent with His own character and purposes.
Omniscience | Latin *omni* + *sciens*, all-knowing | God possesses complete knowledge of everything past, present, and future, including the innermost thoughts and intentions of every human heart.
Omnipresence | | God is fully present everywhere at once, not divided or spread thin but wholly present in every place simultaneously.
Immutability | Latin *immutabilis*, unchangeable | God does not change in His essential character, purposes, or promises.
Mercy | | God's compassionate withholding of deserved punishment.
Grace | | God's unmerited favor toward those who have done nothing to earn it.
Propitiation | | The turning aside of wrath through sacrifice. John uses it of Christ in 1 John 4:10.
:::

## 1. Two Categories, and What They Are For

Theologians have traditionally organized the attributes into two broad groups.

**Communicable attributes** are those God shares, in a limited and creaturely way, with human beings made in His image. Love, holiness, wisdom, and faithfulness belong here. We can reflect them, however imperfectly, in our own character.

**Incommunicable attributes** belong to God alone and cannot be shared with creatures at all. Omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence, and self-existence describe the unique, infinite nature of God rather than anything a finite creature could possess.

:::note What the distinction is not for
It is not meant to fragment God's character into separate compartments. It helps us understand which attributes we are meant to imitate as part of growth in Christlikeness, and which exist to remind us that however much we grow in grace, we remain creatures worshiping a Creator who is fundamentally other than we are.
:::

## 2. Holiness as the Lens

We begin again with holiness, because it colors and shapes every other attribute. When we say God is holy, we mean both that He is entirely separate from sin and that He is the definitive standard of moral purity against which everything else is measured.

Every other attribute must be understood through this lens.

- God's love is **holy** love, never sentimental or careless about sin.
- God's justice is **holy** justice, never harsh or arbitrary but perfectly righteous.

Peter, quoting the ancient command given to Israel, tells believers to be holy in all their conduct, because it is written, be holy, for I am holy (1 Peter 1:15 to 16, echoing Leviticus 11:44 to 45). Holiness is not merely something God possesses. It is something God calls His people to reflect.

## 3. Love

John writes plainly that God is love, and then explains what that love looks like concretely: in this the love of God was manifested toward us, that God has sent His only begotten Son into the world, that we might live through Him, that He loved us and sent His Son to be the propitiation for our sins (1 John 4:8 to 10).

This must be understood correctly. God's love is not a vague feeling of general benevolence toward humanity. It is a costly, purposeful, self-giving love, proven not by sentiment but by sacrifice. Paul makes the same point: God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us (Romans 5:8).

:::quote Chapter Seven
The cross is where God's love and God's justice meet most powerfully, because it was precisely there that sin was fully punished even as sinners were fully forgiven.
:::

## 4. Justice, Mercy, and Grace

God's justice is His commitment to always act rightly and to ensure that wrongdoing is addressed rather than ignored.

It can be tempting, especially in a culture that prizes tolerance above other values, to want a God who is entirely love with no corresponding commitment to justice. But a God who never judged sin would not be more loving. He would be indifferent to the suffering sin causes, indifferent to the cries of the oppressed, indifferent to genuine evil in the world.

:::quote Chapter Seven
God's justice is actually good news, because it means the wrongs of this world will not go forever unaddressed.
:::

Alongside justice, Scripture reveals **mercy**, God's compassionate withholding of deserved punishment, and **grace**, His unmerited favor toward those who have done nothing to earn it. When Moses asked to see God's glory, the Lord proclaimed His own name, describing Himself as merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, while making clear that He would by no means clear the guilty (Exodus 34:6 to 7).

Mercy and justice are not competing attributes that God awkwardly balances. They are both fully expressed, without contradiction, at the cross, where justice was satisfied and mercy was extended at once through the substitutionary death of Christ.

## 5. Wisdom and Faithfulness

God's **wisdom** describes His perfect knowledge of how to accomplish His purposes in the best possible way. His **faithfulness** describes His unwavering reliability to keep every promise He has made.

Malachi records God declaring, I am the Lord, I do not change (Malachi 3:6). This unchanging faithfulness is not a cold, mechanical consistency. It is a warm, dependable steadiness that believers throughout Scripture have found tremendous comfort in. When everything else feels uncertain, when circumstances shift and people disappoint us, God's faithfulness remains the one constant we can build our lives upon without fear of collapse.

## 6. The Incommunicable Attributes

| Attribute | Definition | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Omnipotence | All power, able to accomplish anything consistent with His character and purposes | Unrestrained by any limitation outside Himself |
| Omniscience | Complete knowledge of past, present, and future, including the thoughts of every heart | Matthew 10:29 to 30 |
| Omnipresence | Fully present everywhere at once, wholly present in every place simultaneously | Psalm 139:7 to 10 |
| Immutability | No change in essential character, purposes, or promises | Malachi 3:6 |

Two clarifications matter here.

Omnipresence does not mean God is divided or spread thin across the universe. The psalmist marveled at this, asking where he could go from God's Spirit, or where he could flee from God's presence, and concluding that even in the farthest reaches of the sea, God's hand would still lead him (Psalm 139:7 to 10).

Immutability does not mean God is static or unresponsive. Scripture describes Him relating dynamically and responsively to His creatures within the outworking of His unchanging purposes.

Jesus told the woman at the well that God is Spirit, and that those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth (John 4:24).

## 7. Every Attribute Displayed in One Person

The most important thing to understand about the attributes together is that every one of them finds its clearest, fullest display in Jesus Christ.

Throughout the Gospels we see holiness, love, justice, mercy, wisdom, and power displayed together in one Person: healing the sick with compassion, confronting hypocrisy with righteous anger, forgiving sinners while calling them to repentance, and finally laying down His life in an act that perfectly harmonized justice and mercy in a single, world-altering moment.

If you want to know whether you have understood an attribute correctly, test your understanding against Christ. An account of God's love that Jesus would not recognize is not God's love. An account of God's justice that cannot be seen at Calvary is not God's justice.

## 8. How the Attributes Protect One Another

This section is the heart of the chapter, and the part most worth memorizing.

Each attribute protects and clarifies the others from being misunderstood. Isolate one, and it curdles.

| Attribute isolated | Distortion produced |
|---|---|
| Love, without holiness and justice | Vague sentimentality that treats sin as unimportant and excuses wrongdoing without addressing it |
| Justice, without mercy and grace | Harsh legalism that offers no hope to those who have genuinely failed |
| Sovereignty, without goodness | An arbitrary, even frightening deity whose power cannot be trusted |

:::warn The governing rule
Scripture never presents these attributes in isolation from one another. They belong together as a single, unified portrait, and any theology that emphasizes one attribute at the expense of the others will eventually produce a distorted view of God and, in turn, a distorted Christian life.
:::

This is a diagnostic tool. When you encounter teaching that feels subtly wrong but you cannot say why, ask which attribute has been isolated and which have been quietly dropped.

## 9. Suffering and the Harmony of the Attributes

If God is both perfectly loving and perfectly powerful, why does He allow suffering to continue?

The chapter does not pretend to settle the question in a paragraph, and it points toward a foundational piece of the answer. God's attributes do not operate independently of one another, as though His love occasionally overrides His justice, or His power occasionally overrides His wisdom. They operate together, in perfect harmony, according to purposes not always immediately visible from our limited vantage point.

Joseph's brothers intended evil against him, yet God, without excusing their sin, wove even that evil into a plan that eventually saved their entire family from famine (Genesis 50:20).

:::quote Chapter Seven
The cross itself stands as the ultimate example of this pattern, the greatest apparent evil in human history, the unjust execution of the sinless Son of God, becoming, in God's hands, the very means of the world's redemption.
:::

We are not always given to see, in the moment, how God is weaving suffering into a good purpose. The cross gives us solid ground to trust that He is doing exactly that.

## 10. Attributes That Become a Calling

God's attributes shape the specific character He calls His people to display.

- Because God is **holy**, believers are called to pursue holiness in their conduct.
- Because God is **loving**, believers are called to love sacrificially, even toward those who have wronged them.
- Because God is **just**, believers are called to pursue justice and to refuse complicity with oppression and dishonesty.
- Because God is **merciful**, believers are called to extend forgiveness generously rather than holding onto bitterness.
- Because God is **faithful**, believers are called to keep their word and remain steady in their commitments even when doing so is costly.

Peter reminds us that as He who called us is holy, we also are to be holy in all our conduct (1 Peter 1:15), and the pattern extends to every attribute.

:::quote Chapter Seven
We do not study God's character merely to admire it from a safe distance. We study it because we are being formed, day by day, into its likeness.
:::

## Summary

God's attributes are facets of one unified character rather than items on a list. Communicable attributes such as love and faithfulness are ones we are called to reflect. Incommunicable attributes such as omnipotence and omnipresence remind us we remain creatures. Holiness functions as the lens for all the rest, so that love is holy love and justice is holy justice. Love is proven by the sending of the Son, justice is good news because evil will not go unaddressed forever, and mercy and grace meet justice without contradiction at the cross. The decisive insight of the chapter is that no attribute may be read alone: love isolated becomes sentimentality, justice isolated becomes legalism, sovereignty isolated becomes an arbitrary power that cannot be trusted. Every attribute finds its fullest display in Christ, and every one carries a corresponding calling on the believer being formed into that likeness.

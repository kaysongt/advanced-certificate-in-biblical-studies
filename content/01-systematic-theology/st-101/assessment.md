This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 101 and the whole of *What We Believe, Volume 1: God*. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: **80%**.

## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each. Answer every question. Explanations appear once you select an answer.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q Theology, at its simplest, is defined as which of the following?
+ The study of God
- The defense of Christian belief against objection
- The systematic arrangement of church doctrine
- The interpretation of Scripture
= From the Greek *theos*, God, and *logos*, word or study. Because the definition is that broad, every believer who has ever prayed has already been doing theology.

Q Which of the four reasons theology matters functions as the test of a person's theological health?
+ It leads to worship
- It protects against error
- It strengthens faith
- It shapes Christian living
= If study of God leaves us proud rather than humbled and argumentative rather than adoring, we have missed the point no matter how accurate our conclusions.

Q What can general revelation accomplish?
+ It makes God's eternal power and divine nature known, leaving humanity without excuse
- It communicates the specific means by which sinners are reconciled to God
- It provides sufficient knowledge for salvation apart from preaching
- It is available only to those within the covenant community
= Romans 1:19 to 20. Creation cannot preach the gospel, which is why Paul insists faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17).

Q Inspiration, as distinct from revelation and illumination, refers to what?
+ The process by which God ensured the human authors recorded revelation accurately
- The content God has made known about Himself
- The Spirit's work enabling believers to understand Scripture
- The church's recognition of which books belong in the canon
= Revelation gives the truth, inspiration guarantees it was recorded reliably, illumination opens our eyes to receive it.

Q The word translated *inspiration* in 2 Timothy 3:16 literally means what?
+ God-breathed
- God-given
- God-spoken
- God-ordained
= Scripture did not originate in the imagination of religious thinkers reaching upward. It originated in the breath of God moving outward toward humanity.

Q Which was NOT one of the criteria by which the church recognized the canon?
+ Approval by imperial decree
- Apostolic origin or connection
- Consistency with previously recognized Scripture
- Widespread acceptance and use across different regions
= The church did not invent the canon by vote or decree. It recognized which books already carried the marks of divine inspiration.

Q The sufficiency of Scripture means which of the following?
+ Scripture contains everything necessary for salvation and faithful obedience
- Scripture answers every question a believer might ask, including practical life decisions
- Scripture requires no interpretation because its meaning is always plain
- Scripture is adequate for doctrine but must be supplemented for practice
= It does not mean the Bible tells you which house to buy. It means it gives you everything needed to know God, be saved, and live in a way that pleases Him.

Q What is the single most important interpretive principle taught in Chapter 4?
+ Context governs meaning
- Scripture interprets Scripture
- Every passage points to Christ
- The Spirit illuminates the reader
= A verse pulled from its paragraph is like a brick pulled from a building. All the other principles operate within this one.

Q Reading Scripture without sensitivity to covenantal context produces which two errors?
+ A legalism that reapplies old covenant regulations wholesale, and a dismissal of the Old Testament as obsolete
- An over-reliance on prophecy, and a neglect of the wisdom literature
- Excessive literalism, and excessive allegory
- Private interpretation, and uncritical deference to tradition
= The dietary laws and sacrificial system find their fulfillment in Christ rather than continuing as binding requirements for the church.

Q Genesis 3:15 is called the protoevangelium because it does what?
+ Announces a coming deliverer at the very moment of judgment
- Records the first sacrifice offered for sin
- Contains the first divine promise of land to a chosen people
- Establishes the first covenant between God and humanity
= From humanity's darkest moment, God announced His intention to send a deliverer whose offspring would bruise the serpent's head.

Q On the road to Emmaus, how did Jesus treat the Hebrew Scriptures?
+ Beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, He explained in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself
- He explained a limited set of specific messianic predictions
- He declared them fulfilled and therefore no longer necessary
- He corrected the disciples' reliance on written texts
= Luke 24:27. The Christ-centered reading of the whole Bible is Jesus' own method rather than a later interpretive invention.

Q What does aseity mean?
+ God's existence is not derived from or dependent on anything outside Himself
- God is unchanging in character and purpose
- God is present everywhere at once
- God possesses complete knowledge of all things
= From the Latin *a se*, from himself. God answered Moses at the burning bush with I AM WHO I AM (Exodus 3:14).

Q In Isaiah's throne room vision, which attribute did the seraphim declare three times?
+ Holiness
- Sovereignty
- Love
- Righteousness
= Isaiah 6:3. Holiness is the attribute Scripture emphasizes most when describing God's essential nature.

Q What follows from God's self-sufficiency?
+ He created and redeemed out of overflowing generosity rather than need
- He remains detached from the affairs of His creation
- His love for humanity is conditional on human usefulness
- He required creation to complete the fellowship of the Trinity
= We were never necessary additions to an incomplete God. We are the undeserved recipients of a God whose love simply chose to include us.

Q Which pairing correctly matches an isolated attribute with the distortion it produces?
+ Sovereignty without goodness produces an arbitrary deity whose power cannot be trusted
- Love without holiness produces a harsh legalism
- Justice without mercy produces vague sentimentality
- Holiness without love produces presumption about sin
= Love isolated becomes sentimentality, justice isolated becomes legalism, sovereignty isolated becomes arbitrary power. Each attribute protects the others.

Q Where do God's justice and mercy meet without contradiction?
+ At the cross, where sin was fully punished even as sinners were fully forgiven
- At Sinai, in the giving of the law
- At the final judgment, applied separately to two groups
- They remain in unresolved tension throughout Scripture
= Exodus 34:6 to 7 holds both in a single self-description: merciful and gracious, yet by no means clearing the guilty.

Q Which of these is an incommunicable attribute?
+ Omnipresence
- Faithfulness
- Wisdom
- Love
= Communicable attributes such as love, wisdom, and faithfulness can be reflected by creatures made in God's image. Omnipresence, omnipotence, omniscience, and self-existence cannot.

Q Modalism is best described as which error?
+ Teaching that God is one Person appearing in three modes at different times
- Teaching that there are three separate gods cooperating together
- Teaching that the Son is a created being subordinate to the Father
- Teaching that the Spirit is an impersonal force rather than a Person
= Modalism cannot account for the baptism of Jesus, where Father, Son, and Spirit are present and distinct at the same moment.

Q What is significant about the phrase "in the name" in Matthew 28:19?
+ It is singular, followed by three distinct Persons, reflecting one divine name shared by three
- It is plural, confirming three distinct divine identities
- It indicates that baptism is performed by delegated authority
- It refers to the covenant name revealed to Moses at the burning bush
= Jesus does not say in the names, as though addressing three separate deities. The grammatical detail carries real theological weight.

Q According to Ephesians 1:4 to 5, when did God's fatherly intention toward believers begin?
+ Before the foundation of the world
- At the moment of each believer's conversion
- At the incarnation of the Son
- At Pentecost, when the Spirit of adoption was given
= Our believing did not create God's fatherly intention. It was the moment we entered a love and purpose set upon us from before time began.

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each, 30 points total. Answer each in 100 to 150 words. Cite at least one passage per answer.

**B1.** Explain the difference between general and special revelation, stating precisely what each accomplishes and what neither can do without the other. Why does this distinction make missions necessary rather than optional?

**B2.** Define plenary verbal inspiration. Then explain what the doctrine does *not* claim, using the differences between Luke's, John's, and Isaiah's writing styles.

**B3.** A believer applies Jeremiah 29:11 to a job application, treating it as a personal guarantee of success. Walk through the interpretive method taught in Topic 3: what the passage meant originally, what principle legitimately carries across, and where the popular application goes wrong.

**B4.** Explain why the textbook insists that holiness is the lens through which every other attribute must be read. Illustrate with two attributes, showing how each changes when holiness is removed.

**B5.** State the doctrine of the Trinity accurately. Then identify modalism and tritheism, and explain in one sentence each what biblical evidence they fail to account for.

:::note Marking guide for Section B
Full marks require: an accurate definition, at least one correctly cited passage, and evidence that the student has understood *why* the point matters rather than only *what* it says. Answers that reproduce the textbook wording without demonstrating comprehension receive at most half marks.
:::

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> The textbook argues that theology which stops at accurate description without moving into adoration has fallen short of its purpose. Take one truth about God covered in this course, either from His nature in Topic 4 or His attributes in Topic 5. Show from at least three passages how Scripture reveals it, explain how it connects to at least one other truth covered in the course, and describe honestly how a settled confidence in it would change a specific area of your life or ministry.

You may not choose a truth you have already used in the course assignment.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Accuracy of doctrine and correct use of at least three passages | 10 |
| Coherence of the connections drawn between doctrines | 8 |
| Clarity and structure of the argument | 6 |
| Honesty and specificity of the application | 6 |

:::warn What loses marks
Generalities. "This truth has changed my life" is not an application. Name the situation, name the previous response, name the changed response. The marker is looking for evidence that a doctrine has been thought about rather than admired.
:::

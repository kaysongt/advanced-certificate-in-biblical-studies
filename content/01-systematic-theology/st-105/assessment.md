This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 105 and the whole of *What We Believe, Volume 5: The Church*. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: **70%**.

## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q What does *ekklesia* mean?
+ A gathering called out from the general population for a particular purpose
- A consecrated building for religious assembly
- A council of appointed religious leaders
- A regular meeting for instruction in doctrine
= In the ancient Greek world it described a legislative assembly, citizens summoned out of their homes and daily routines.

Q Which of the following IS the Church?
+ The people themselves, called out, redeemed, and united to Christ and one another
- The building where believers gather
- The denomination organizing believers for ministry
- The weekly worship service
= Take away the building, cancel the service, dissolve the denomination, and the Church still exists.

Q What does the royal priesthood of all believers establish?
+ Direct access to God for every believer, with no mediating class of clergy
- A hierarchy of spiritual maturity within the congregation
- The abolition of teaching and pastoral oversight
- The transfer of Levitical duties to church leaders
= Under the old covenant access was mediated and restricted. Under the new, every member has priestly access and priestly responsibility.

Q Which two temptations does Paul confront with the Body metaphor?
+ Self-disqualification, and contempt for less visible gifts
- Doctrinal error, and moral compromise
- Isolation from the world, and conformity to it
- Legalism, and licence
= The foot saying it does not belong, and the eye saying to the hand, I do not need you.

Q What is Paul's teaching about the weaker-seeming parts of the Body?
+ They are indispensable, and are treated with special honour
- They should be strengthened until they match the others
- They serve a subordinate supporting function
- They will eventually be replaced as the body matures
= This is a remarkable inversion of how the world assigns value, which elevates the visible, the articulate, and the powerful.

Q What sequence does Ephesians 5 establish about Christ and the Church?
+ Christ loved her first, and His love is what makes her holy
- The Church purified herself and was then received by Christ
- Christ's love is granted in proportion to the Church's faithfulness
- The two developed together over redemptive history
= We are not working toward Christ's love. We are working from it.

Q What does the bride's fine linen represent in Revelation 19?
+ The righteous acts of God's holy people
- The imputed righteousness of Christ alone
- The doctrinal purity of the church
- The testimony of the martyrs
= Our faithfulness in this life is, in some mysterious way, woven into the very garments the Church wears at her wedding.

Q What did *household* mean in the ancient world?
+ The entire structure of daily life, including shared meals, resources, and responsibility for one another's welfare
- A term of affection for close friendships
- The biological family under one roof
- A legal unit for property and inheritance
= To call the Church a household was to say believers owe one another the practical, sacrificial care family members owe each other.

Q How does Galatians 6:2 frame burden-bearing?
+ As the fulfillment of the law of Christ
- As an optional expression of exceptional compassion
- As a duty of church leaders toward members
- As a temporary provision during crisis
= Burden-bearing requires proximity. You cannot carry a burden you do not know about.

Q How does the textbook describe the friction of church family life?
+ As a primary instrument God uses to shape us into Christ's likeness
- As evidence of an unhealthy congregation
- As an unfortunate cost to be minimized
- As grounds for seeking a more compatible church
= You cannot practice forgiveness toward someone whose failures never affect you because your involvement is too shallow.

Q How does Paul define worship in Romans 12:1?
+ The offering of one's entire life as a living sacrifice
- The corporate singing of praise
- The observance of the ordinances
- The exercise of spiritual gifts in the assembly
= Under the old covenant sacrifices were dead animals on an altar. Under the new, the sacrifice is a living person.

Q What does Hebrews 13:15 to 16 place alongside the sacrifice of praise?
+ Doing good and sharing with others
- Fasting and self-denial
- Public confession of sin
- Faithful attendance at gatherings
= Singing God's praises and sharing resources with someone in need are both counted as sacrifices offered to God.

Q What does the structure of the Lord's Prayer establish?
+ Worship first, then God's purposes, then honest dependence and confession
- That prayers should be brief and specific
- That a fixed verbal formula should be used
- That corporate prayer takes priority over private prayer
= Prayer that begins with a list of requests, treating God primarily as a source of provision, misses the relational depth Jesus models.

Q What does baptism accomplish?
+ It is the appointed sign and public declaration of a salvation received through faith
- It produces forgiveness of sins through the water
- It completes what faith begins
- It confers formal membership in a congregation
= Salvation comes through faith in Christ's finished work, not through any ritual, however sacred.

Q What is the forward orientation of the Lord's Supper?
+ It proclaims the Lord's death until He comes
- It anticipates the believer's individual resurrection only
- It points toward the next observance
- It has no forward orientation
= Every observance holds together His death accomplished, His presence known, and His return anticipated.

Q What is the difference between a convert and a disciple?
+ A convert has made a decision; a disciple has entered a lifelong process of learning to follow Jesus
- A convert is recent; a disciple is mature
- A convert believes privately; a disciple is baptized publicly
- The terms are interchangeable in the New Testament
= Jesus commands His followers to make disciples, and the commission's goal is teaching them to obey.

Q What does 2 Timothy 2:2 establish?
+ A four-generation chain of discipleship carried by ordinary reliable believers
- That only trained theologians should teach
- That discipleship requires a formal church programme
- That each believer is responsible only for their own growth
= Reliable people, not necessarily formally trained theologians, are entrusted with passing on what they received.

Q What is most striking about Paul's qualifications for overseers?
+ How ordinary they are, requiring tested character rather than charisma or credentials
- How restrictive they are, disqualifying most candidates
- How much they emphasize theological education
- How heavily they weight public speaking ability
= Proven character, demonstrated over time in ordinary family and community relationships, matters more than platform ability.

Q What assumption does the logic of Romans 10 dismantle?
+ That exemplary living substitutes for actual proclamation of the gospel
- That evangelism requires theological training
- That only ordained ministers may preach
- That the gospel must be preached in a formal setting
= A godly life adorns the gospel and removes obstacles, but someone, at some point, has to speak.

Q What third way does Jesus pray for in John 17?
+ Genuine engagement with the world combined with genuine distinctiveness from its values
- Withdrawal from cultural influence into protected communities
- Transformation of institutions from positions of political power
- Neutrality on contested cultural questions
= He does not pray that they be taken out of the world but that they be protected from the evil one, following the incarnational pattern of His own sending.
:::

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

**B1.** Explain the four images of the Church covered in this course: called-out people, Body, Bride, and household. State what each contributes that the others do not.

**B2.** Explain what worship means in the fuller sense Romans 12:1 requires. Then show how Hebrews 13:15 to 16 links praise to generosity, and what that implies for a congregation's practice.

**B3.** State precisely what baptism does and does not accomplish, and explain the theological meaning Paul gives it in Romans 6.

**B4.** Explain the servant-shepherd model of leadership using Mark 10, Acts 20, and 1 Peter 5. Then explain what Ephesians 4 establishes about the purpose of leadership.

**B5.** Distinguish the Church from the Kingdom without collapsing either into the other. Use the embassy image and Romans 14:17 in your answer.

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> Dr. Kay writes that the Church is not an organization you join but a people you become, and he describes a loneliness that afflicts even regular attenders. Using at least four passages from this course, explain what Scripture actually requires of membership in the household of God beyond attendance. Then assess one congregation you know well against that standard, naming specifically what would have to change structurally rather than merely aspirationally.

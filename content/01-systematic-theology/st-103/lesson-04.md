There is a difference between a guest and a resident, and that difference sits at the centre of what makes the New Covenant so remarkable. A guest visits, stays for a time, and then leaves. A resident lives somewhere permanently, unpacking fully, settling in, treating the space as genuinely their own.

Under the Old Covenant the Spirit's presence with individuals resembled a series of visits, powerful but often temporary. Under the New Covenant, the Spirit does not visit believers. He moves in.

This lesson also takes up the subject that has generated more confusion, and more division, among sincere believers than any other aspect of the Spirit's ministry.

:::objectives By the end of this lesson you will be able to
1. Explain the temple image Paul applies to believers and its implications for the body.
2. Explain the Spirit as guarantee and as seal, and their bearing on assurance.
3. Explain the Spirit's internal witness confirming adoption.
4. Distinguish the baptism of the Spirit from the ongoing fullness of the Spirit.
5. Explain the purpose for which the Spirit's power was given.
6. Describe how to pursue fullness without manufacturing an experience, and identify the two ditches to avoid.
:::

## Key Terms

:::terms
Indwelling | | The permanent residence of the Spirit within every believer, established at conversion.
Temple | Greek *naos* | The innermost sanctuary where a deity was believed to dwell. Paul applies the word to ordinary believers and to the physical body.
Guarantee | Greek *arrabon* | A down payment, an initial installment that both promises and guarantees a larger payment still to come.
Seal | Greek *sphragis* | A mark of ownership and protection, like a wax seal confirming both sender and integrity. Believers are sealed until the day of redemption.
Baptism of the Spirit | | The one-time act by which every believer is baptized into union with Christ and His body. Universal among believers.
Fullness of the Spirit | | The ongoing, repeated experience of the Spirit's controlling influence and empowering, commanded in the present continuous in Ephesians 5:18.
:::

## 1. A Temple, Not a Visitor Centre

Paul makes an astonishing claim directly to the Corinthian church, a congregation not exactly known for its spiritual maturity, riddled as it was with division, immorality, and confusion. Do you not know that you are the temple of God and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?

He is not describing an aspiration for especially devoted believers to work toward. He is describing the present reality of every believer in that troubled congregation, and by extension every believer since.

The word *temple* is not chosen casually. In the ancient world a temple was the specific location where a deity was believed to dwell, the holiest possible space. Paul applies that exact word to ordinary believers, some of whom were at that very moment tolerating sin he had to confront directly.

He returns to the image with sharper focus later in the same letter, and notice the logic of his argument. He does not appeal to abstract rules about morality. He appeals to identity. Because the Spirit lives within your body, your body itself becomes sacred space, and how you use it becomes a matter of stewardship rather than mere personal preference.

:::quote Chapter Six
You are not a soul temporarily housed in a disposable physical shell, waiting to be discarded when something more spiritual comes along. Your body, indwelt by the Spirit, is itself part of the sacred reality of your identity in Christ.
:::

## 2. The Down Payment

Paul offers another image that deepens our understanding. He describes the Spirit as a *guarantee*, a word that in its original context referred to a down payment, an initial installment that both promises and guarantees a larger payment still to come.

Think carefully about what this means. The Spirit's indwelling presence right now is not the full measure of what God has for us. It is a genuine, substantial installment, and it points forward to something greater still, the full inheritance believers will receive when Christ returns and creation itself is renewed.

:::quote Chapter Six
If the down payment already includes access to God's presence, comfort in trial, power for holy living, and assurance of belonging, imagine what the full inheritance holds. The Spirit's indwelling is not a consolation prize while we wait for something better. It is the first, guaranteed installment of glory.
:::

## 3. Sealed and Secure

The image of sealing carries its own weight. In the ancient world a seal marked ownership and guaranteed protection, the way a wax seal on a letter confirmed both who sent it and that it had not been tampered with.

When Paul describes believers as sealed with the Holy Spirit, he is describing both ownership and security, and he says the sealing lasts until the day of redemption.

:::note For those who struggle with assurance
This seal is not periodically renewed based on your performance. It was placed the moment you believed, and it remains until the day your redemption is finally completed at Christ's return. It does not depend on your consistency. It depends on Christ's finished work, applied to you and confirmed by the Spirit's abiding presence.
:::

## 4. Confirming Our Adoption

Romans adds a dimension that touches the deepest emotional needs of the human heart. The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God.

This is not merely a doctrinal fact we accept intellectually. It is an internal witness, a Spirit-given confidence that settles into the believer's own awareness.

He does not merely inform us of our adoption from the outside, the way a legal document might. He confirms it from within, testifying alongside our own spirit, so that belonging to God becomes something we know rather than merely something we have been told.

## 5. A Presence That Cannot Be Outgrown

Some believers unconsciously assume that as they mature spiritually they somehow need the Spirit's presence less, as though indwelling were primarily a beginner's provision.

This assumption has no support in Scripture. Paul wrote his teaching on the indwelling Spirit not to spiritual novices but to established churches.

:::quote Chapter Six
The most mature believers you will ever meet are not those who have learned to need the Spirit less, but those who have learned, through years of experience, how thoroughly and continuously they need Him.
:::

## 6. The Promise of Power

Before His ascension Jesus told His disciples not to depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promise of the Father, for John baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now.

He then expanded on what this baptism would accomplish. You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth (Acts 1:8).

Notice the connection between the Spirit's coming and empowered witness. This was not primarily about a private religious experience, though it certainly touched them deeply. It was about equipping ordinary men and women, most without formal training or social standing, to carry the gospel with power.

## 7. One Baptism, Many Fillings

This is the distinction that resolves most of the confusion surrounding this subject.

At Pentecost the believers were **filled** with the Holy Spirit. The same pattern appears repeatedly through Acts. After Peter and John were released from custody, the place where the believers were assembled was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the word of God with boldness (Acts 4:31). That was not their first encounter with the Spirit. It was a fresh filling, given in response to a specific need for courage.

| Baptism of the Spirit | Fullness of the Spirit |
|---|---|
| One-time | Repeated |
| Universal among believers | Varies with yieldedness |
| Establishes relationship and identity | Determines how much of that identity you walk in |
| 1 Corinthians 12:13 | Ephesians 5:18 |

Paul tells the Corinthians plainly that by one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free. This baptism is universal among believers, part of what it means to belong to the body of Christ, not a special experience reserved for a select group who have met additional conditions.

Ephesians captures the ongoing dimension precisely. Do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation, but be filled with the Spirit. The grammar carries a continuous sense, closer to *keep on being filled*. Just as a person under the influence of wine is progressively controlled by it, believers are called to be progressively, continually controlled and influenced by the Spirit.

:::quote Chapter Seven
Believers do not need to wonder anxiously whether they possess the Spirit's baptism, since Scripture identifies that as a settled reality for everyone in Christ. But believers can, and should, examine whether they are walking in the fullness Paul describes.
:::

## 8. Power for Witness, Not Performance

It is worth returning to the purpose Jesus attached to this empowerment, because it protects us from a subtle distortion. The power of the Spirit was never given primarily for personal spiritual experience, however meaningful such experiences might be. It was given for witness, for the advance of the gospel, for the building up of the church, and for the glory of Christ.

Look at how the early church actually used this empowerment. Peter preached with boldness before crowds and councils that could have imprisoned or killed him, and did in fact imprison him more than once. Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, testified to Christ even as he was being stoned. The believers in Antioch, worshipping and fasting, heard the Spirit say, Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul.

:::warn When it goes sideways
When the empowering presence of the Spirit becomes primarily about our own sensations, our own spiritual resumes, or our own status among other believers, something has gone sideways from the New Testament's actual concern. In every biblical case the Spirit's power moved outward, toward mission and witness, rather than inward toward private spiritual satisfaction.
:::

## 9. Pursuing Fullness Without Manufacturing It

How should a believer today pursue the fullness of the Spirit? Not by chasing a particular emotional experience or assuming fullness always looks identical from person to person.

Scripture consistently connects fullness to yieldedness, to a posture of ongoing surrender and dependence rather than a technique for producing a feeling. The believers in Acts who were filled were, without exception, people already marked by prayer, by devotion to the word, and by genuine faith.

Practically this means three things.

1. Creating space through prayer that is honest and unhurried, and through Scripture that is not merely studied but actually obeyed.
2. Examining your life for areas of persistent resistance, habits or attitudes you have quietly decided are non-negotiable, since these are precisely where believers most commonly grieve or quench the Spirit's work.
3. Asking, honestly and often, for the Spirit to fill you afresh, trusting that a Father who delights to give good gifts will not withhold His Spirit from those who ask.

## 10. Two Ditches

Every generation of believers seems to find its way into one of two ditches on this subject.

The **first ditch** treats the baptism and fullness of the Spirit as an emotional performance, chasing sensation and equating volume or intensity with genuine spiritual depth.

The **second ditch**, often a reaction against the first, grows so cautious about excess that it quietly stops expecting the Spirit's power to show up in any tangible way at all, reducing the Christian life to intellectual assent.

:::quote Chapter Seven
Scripture avoids both ditches. It expects genuine, sometimes dramatic encounters with the Spirit's power, the kind that shook a room and launched a global movement, while never treating those encounters as ends in themselves, detached from character, mission, and the ordinary, sustained obedience of daily discipleship.
:::

Expect the Spirit's power. Do not manufacture it. Let it flow, as it always has, toward bold witness and Christlike character rather than toward spectacle for its own sake.

## Reflection

Identify one specific area where you currently feel underpowered: a conversation you have been avoiding, a habit you cannot break, a fear that keeps you silent. Bring that specific situation to the Spirit and ask to be filled afresh for it. Do not wait for an emotional signal before believing He has answered. Step forward in obedience and watch for the courage that so often arrives once you are already moving.

## Summary

Under the New Covenant the Spirit is a resident rather than a guest. Paul calls even immature believers a temple, and applies that word to the physical body, grounding holiness in identity rather than in rules. The Spirit is given as a guarantee, a genuine down payment on a greater inheritance, and as a seal marking ownership and security until the day of redemption. He bears witness within, so that belonging to God is known rather than merely believed. He is not a beginner's provision to be outgrown. On the much-disputed question of baptism and fullness, the textbook draws a clean line: one baptism, universal to every believer and establishing identity, and many fillings, repeated and connected to ongoing yieldedness. That power was given for witness rather than for private experience, and it is pursued through surrender rather than technique. Avoid both ditches. Expect His power, and do not manufacture it. Lesson 5 turns to what He grows and what He gives.

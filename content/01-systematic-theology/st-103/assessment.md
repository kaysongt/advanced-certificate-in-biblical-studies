This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 103 and the whole of *What We Believe, Volume 3: The Holy Spirit*. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: **80%**.

## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q Which statement best describes the Holy Spirit?
+ The third Person of the Trinity, fully God, distinct in Person yet undivided in essence
- A divine power or influence proceeding from the Father
- The presence of God experienced emotionally during worship
- The highest of created beings, serving the Father and the Son
= Almost every confusion about the Spirit's ministry traces back to getting this starting point wrong.

Q Which fact most directly establishes the Spirit's personhood?
+ He can be grieved, which requires emotion capable of being wounded
- He is described as wind and fire in Scripture
- He is present everywhere at once
- He was active at creation
= You cannot grieve a force. You cannot wound electricity. Grief belongs to relationship, and relationship belongs to persons.

Q How does Acts 5 establish the Spirit's deity?
+ Peter tells Ananias he lied to the Holy Spirit and, in the same breath, that he lied to God
- The Spirit struck Ananias and Sapphira dead
- The apostles acted with the Spirit's delegated authority
- The believers held all things in common by the Spirit's direction
= Peter used the two terms as perfect synonyms. The equation required no defense because the early church already understood the Spirit to be divine.

Q What does the Greek word behind *Helper* convey?
+ One called alongside to assist, defend, and strengthen
- One sent ahead as a messenger
- One appointed to teach and correct
- One who intercedes only in times of crisis
= It is a legal and relational term, used for a friend who stands with you in a courtroom or walks beside you through hardship.

Q What was the Old Testament pattern of the Spirit's activity with individuals?
+ He came upon select people for specific tasks, and His presence could be withdrawn
- He indwelt every faithful Israelite permanently
- He was active only through the written law
- He worked exclusively through the priesthood
= Bezalel, Gideon, Samson, Saul, and David all illustrate empowerment tied to task and moment rather than permanent indwelling.

Q Why did David plead that God not take His Holy Spirit from him?
+ He had watched the Spirit depart from Saul and knew it was possible under the covenant he lived under
- He feared losing his poetic and musical gift
- He was expressing poetic exaggeration rather than genuine concern
- He was praying on behalf of the nation rather than himself
= He was not being dramatic. He was being honest about the terms of the covenant he lived under.

Q Which prophet most clearly promised the Spirit dwelling *within* God's people?
+ Ezekiel
- Isaiah
- Micah
- Malachi
= Ezekiel 36:26 to 27 promises a new heart, removal of the heart of stone, and God putting His Spirit within His people.

Q What does John 7:39 establish about the indwelling Spirit?
+ It was secured by and dependent on the completion of Christ's work
- It was available to Old Testament believers in the same measure
- It was delayed by the disciples' lack of faith
- It would be given only to the apostles
= The Spirit had not yet been given because Jesus was not yet glorified. The indwelling is a gift secured by the cross rather than given independently of it.

Q What changed at Pentecost?
+ Not the Spirit's character, but the terms of His dwelling
- The Spirit began to exist as a distinct Person
- The Spirit became fully divine
- The Spirit ceased His Old Testament work entirely
= He came upon select individuals under the Old Covenant. He now indwells every believer permanently, without distinction of status.

Q What produces the new birth?
+ The sovereign work of the Spirit alone
- Sincere repentance combined with moral reform
- Religious instruction leading to intellectual assent
- Baptism administered by the church
= No amount of law-keeping, ritual observance, or moral effort generates spiritual life. It is given, and it is given by the Spirit alone.

Q What does Paul's phrase *new creation* establish about conversion?
+ God creates something genuinely new rather than improving what was already there
- God restores the original condition of Adam
- God begins a gradual moral improvement
- God changes legal standing without inward change
= Salvation is not God sanding down the rough edges of the old version of you until something acceptable emerges.

Q How does faith relate to regeneration?
+ Faith receives what the Spirit accomplishes; it does not produce it
- Faith causes regeneration through an act of human will
- Faith and regeneration are identical
- Faith becomes unnecessary after regeneration
= The Spirit opens spiritually blind eyes, and in response a person believes. The believing is genuinely theirs; the capacity to believe rightly is His gift.

Q Why does Paul call believers a temple?
+ Because a temple is where a deity dwells, and the Spirit dwells in every believer
- Because believers should treat church buildings as sacred
- Because believers replace the sacrificial system
- Because believers gather corporately for worship
= He applied the word to the Corinthian believers while confronting their sin in the same letter, describing a present reality rather than an aspiration.

Q What does it mean that the Spirit is a *guarantee*?
+ He is a down payment that promises and secures a greater inheritance still to come
- He is a conditional pledge dependent on continued obedience
- He is a temporary provision for the apostolic age
- He is proof offered to unbelievers of God's existence
= If the down payment already includes God's presence, comfort, power, and assurance, the full inheritance is greater still.

Q What distinguishes the baptism of the Spirit from the fullness of the Spirit?
+ Baptism is one-time and universal to believers; fullness is repeated and tied to yieldedness
- Baptism is for the mature; fullness is for new believers
- Baptism confers gifts; fullness confers fruit
- They are two names for the same event
= Ephesians 5:18 carries a continuous sense, closer to keep on being filled, while 1 Corinthians 12:13 describes a settled reality for everyone in Christ.

Q For what purpose was the Spirit's power given?
+ For empowered witness and the advance of the gospel
- For the personal spiritual satisfaction of believers
- For authenticating the authority of church leaders
- For protection from persecution
= In every biblical case the Spirit's power moved outward toward mission rather than inward toward private spiritual satisfaction.

Q Why does Paul write *fruit* rather than *fruits*?
+ Because it is one unified harvest from a single root rather than nine separate achievements
- Because only one quality appears in each believer
- Because the list is representative rather than complete
- Because Greek lacks a plural form of the word
= Paul is not listing achievements a believer might collect independently. The fruit grows together.

Q Which statement correctly distinguishes fruit from gifts?
+ Every believer is called to grow the full range of fruit; gifts are distributed differently to different believers
- Fruit is given at conversion; gifts are earned through service
- Fruit is inward; gifts are outward
- Fruit belongs to leaders; gifts belong to members
= There is no believer meant to specialize in love while neglecting self-control. Gifts, by contrast, are apportioned as the Spirit wills.

Q What is Paul arguing by placing his teaching on love between two chapters on gifts?
+ Love is the frame within which every gift must operate to have lasting value
- Love is the greatest of the spiritual gifts
- Gifts should be abandoned in favour of love
- Only mature believers should exercise gifts
= Gifts without love become performances. Love without gifts still accomplishes genuine good.

Q What pattern did the Jerusalem council establish?
+ Prayerful communal discernment that seeks and expects the Spirit's guidance
- Deference to the most senior apostle present
- Resolution by majority vote
- Postponement of contested questions
= They wrote that it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us, claiming the Spirit's guidance rather than independent human authority.
:::

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

**B1.** Demonstrate the personhood of the Holy Spirit using three distinct pieces of scriptural evidence. Then explain why this matters practically for how a believer relates to Him.

**B2.** Trace the Spirit's activity through the Old Testament using three specific individuals. Then state precisely what changed at Pentecost and what did not.

**B3.** Explain regeneration as the Spirit's work, using Ephesians 2 to establish the human condition beforehand. Then explain the role faith plays without collapsing it into regeneration.

**B4.** Distinguish the baptism of the Spirit from the fullness of the Spirit. Explain how each is received, and describe the two ditches believers fall into on this subject.

**B5.** Distinguish fruit from gifts across at least three dimensions. Then explain, using 1 Corinthians 13, why gifts exercised without love amount to nothing.

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> Dr. Kay writes that if you miss what changed at Pentecost, you will spend your whole Christian life waiting for something you have already been given. Choose one area of Christian experience covered in this course, either assurance, sanctification, prayer, or ministry. Show from at least three passages how the New Covenant reality of the indwelling Spirit bears on that area, describe a specific way believers commonly live below that reality, and explain how you would teach the difference to a congregation.

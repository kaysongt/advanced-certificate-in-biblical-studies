Paul gives a deceptively simple instruction in Galatians, one that could easily be read past without absorbing its full weight. Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh.

Notice the order. He does not say stop gratifying the desires of the flesh, and eventually you will learn to walk by the Spirit. He reverses the order entirely. Walking by the Spirit comes first, and the natural result is a life no longer dominated by the flesh's demands.

:::objectives By the end of this topic you will be able to
1. Explain the ordering of Galatians 5:16 and why so much of the Christian life is approached backward.
2. Describe the internal conflict Paul acknowledges and explain why feeling it is not evidence of failure.
3. Explain the renewal of the mind as the Spirit's means of sanctification.
4. Explain the difference between being led and being driven, and how sensitivity to the Spirit's leading grows.
5. Distinguish grieving the Spirit from quenching the Spirit.
6. Describe the Spirit's active leadership of the Church in formation, mission, unity, and the equipping of leaders.
:::

## Key Terms

:::terms
Walking in the Spirit | Greek *peripateo*, to walk about | The continuous, moment-by-moment rhythm of dependence on and yieldedness to the Spirit.
Flesh | Greek *sarx* | In Paul's usage, the fallen human nature with its desires, not the physical body as such.
Renewal of the Mind | | The Spirit's reshaping of how a believer thinks, interprets circumstances, and desires, chiefly through Scripture.
Grieving the Spirit | | Wounding a Person capable of genuine emotion, typically through sin, bitterness, or persistent disobedience.
Quenching the Spirit | | Suppressing or ignoring His promptings until they grow faint and difficult to discern. An image drawn from extinguishing a flame.
Jerusalem Council | | The gathering in Acts 15 that resolved the Gentile controversy, describing its conclusion as seeming good to the Holy Spirit and to them.
:::

## 1. The Order Matters

So much of the Christian life gets approached backward. Many believers spend enormous energy trying to suppress sinful desires directly, through willpower, through guilt, through elaborate systems of accountability, and find themselves exhausted and defeated.

:::quote Chapter Ten
Paul's instruction offers a different path entirely, one centred not on suppression but on direction. Walk by the Spirit, moment by moment, choice by choice, and the flesh's grip weakens not because you fought it into submission but because you were walking somewhere else.
:::

## 2. An Honest Account of the Conflict

Paul does not pretend this walk is effortless or automatic. The desires of the flesh are against the Spirit, and the desires of the Spirit are against the flesh, for these are opposed to each other, to keep you from doing the things you want to do.

This is honest, sobering language. Even after regeneration, even indwelt by the Spirit, believers experience a real internal tension between competing desires.

:::note If you feel this acutely
It is not evidence that something has gone wrong in your spiritual life. It is evidence that you are exactly where every believer finds themselves this side of glory, caught in the ongoing process of sanctification. The question is not whether you will feel this tension. You will. The question is which side you will yield to in each specific moment of choice.
:::

Paul catalogues the works of the flesh with unflinching specificity, and warns that those who make a practice of such things will not inherit the kingdom of God. This is not describing a believer who stumbles into sin and repents, but a pattern of unrepentant life given over entirely to the flesh's demands.

## 3. Renewing the Mind

Romans adds a crucial dimension focused on the mind rather than immediate action. Those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit set their minds on the things of the Spirit. To set the mind on the flesh is death, but to set the mind on the Spirit is life and peace.

This is language about ongoing orientation, not a single decisive moment. What occupies your thoughts in unguarded moments, what you turn to for comfort, what shapes your imagination and your desires, these reveal which mindset currently governs your inner life.

:::quote Chapter Ten
Sanctification is not merely about controlling outward behavior. It reaches down into the way you think, the way you interpret your circumstances, the assumptions and desires that operate beneath conscious awareness.
:::

This is why Scripture holds such a central place in the life of the Spirit-filled believer. The Spirit's ordinary means of renewing a believer's mind is the word of God, read, studied, memorized, meditated on, until it begins to reshape not just what you know but how you actually think and feel.

## 4. Led, Not Driven

Paul offers a phrase in Romans that deserves attention because of how gently it describes the Spirit's relationship with believers. For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God.

*Led*, not driven, not forced, not manipulated. Leadership implies a relationship of trust and voluntary following rather than coercion. The Spirit's guidance in daily life typically operates through gentle promptings, quiet convictions, an inner sense of peace or unease that steers a believer's choices.

Learning to recognize this leading takes practice and grows with familiarity, much like learning to recognize a close friend's voice among many others in a crowded room. The promptings are often quiet, easily missed by a believer whose attention is elsewhere, easily suppressed by a believer accustomed to overriding inner conviction.

:::quote Chapter Ten
They grow clearer with practice, with a life increasingly attentive to His presence, and with a willingness to actually obey what He prompts, even in small matters, since obedience in small things builds the sensitivity needed for larger ones.
:::

## 5. Grieving and Quenching

Paul uses two distinct images for how believers hinder the Spirit's work.

| | Grieving the Spirit | Quenching the Spirit |
|---|---|---|
| **Image** | Wounding a Person capable of emotion | Extinguishing a flame |
| **How** | Sin, bitterness, persistent disobedience contrary to His character | Suppressing or ignoring His promptings until they grow faint |
| **Text** | Ephesians 4:30 | 1 Thessalonians 5:19 |

Neither describes losing the Spirit's presence altogether, since His indwelling remains permanent regardless. Both describe a real, painful diminishment of the vibrant, responsive relationship He intends to have with you.

Walking in the Spirit means paying attention to both warnings: staying tender to conviction when you have grieved Him, and staying responsive to promptings you might otherwise be tempted to quench through neglect or fear.

## 6. A Rhythm, Not a Destination

Colossians offers a summary image. As you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him, rooted and built up in Him and established in the faith.

The word *walk* implies a continuous rhythm rather than a single decisive step taken once and never revisited. Just as you first received Christ by faith rather than by works, the entire Christian life that follows continues on that same foundation.

Some days this rhythm feels natural and close. Other days it feels like a genuine fight against competing desires. Both experiences belong to the normal Christian life.

## 7. A Community Formed by the Spirit

The church was born on a single day, in a single city, with a sound like rushing wind, but it did not stay small or contained for long. What began with a gathering of frightened disciples became, within a single generation, a movement that reached from Jerusalem to Rome.

Luke's description of the earliest church reveals a community shaped from its foundation by the Spirit's presence: devoted to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers, marked by awe, wonders and signs, and radical generosity.

Notice how the passage concludes. The Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved. Growth, even numerical growth, is attributed to the Lord's activity through the Spirit rather than primarily to human strategy.

:::warn What this does and does not say about programmes
Programmes, structures, and strategic planning are not unimportant, and Scripture nowhere condemns thoughtful organization. But the New Testament consistently locates the true source of the church's vitality in the Spirit's active work. A church can have excellent programming and remain spiritually lifeless if it has quenched or ignored the Spirit's leadership. A church with far fewer resources can flourish remarkably when it remains genuinely attentive and responsive to Him.
:::

## 8. The Spirit Who Sends

One of the most striking scenes in Acts occurs at Antioch, a congregation of prophets and teachers worshipping the Lord and fasting together. As they worshipped, the Holy Spirit said, Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them. The church responded by fasting and praying, laying hands on the two men, and sending them off.

This establishes something crucial about how the Spirit leads the church's mission. He is not merely a resource the church draws upon after human leaders have already decided on a strategic direction. He is an active voice within the gathered community, directing, calling, and sending.

:::quote Chapter Eleven
The church at Antioch did not invent the missionary journey that would become one of the most consequential undertakings in church history. They received it, discerned it together in a posture of worship and fasting, and obeyed it. The Spirit initiated. The church responded.
:::

His leadership operated sometimes through open doors and sometimes, notably, through closed ones, as when the Spirit forbade Paul from preaching in one region and redirected him toward Macedonia.

## 9. Preserving Unity Amid Real Difference

The early church did not remain free of conflict for long. A significant dispute arose over whether Gentile believers needed to be circumcised and keep the Mosaic law to be genuinely saved, a controversy serious enough to threaten the unity of the entire movement.

The leaders gathered in Jerusalem, and after considerable debate reached a resolution they described in remarkable terms: it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us.

The council did not claim independent human authority to settle the dispute. They claimed the Spirit's own guidance, discerned together through prayerful deliberation over Scripture and the testimony of what God was visibly doing among the Gentiles.

This offers an enduring pattern for how the church navigates genuine disagreement: not through raw exercises of institutional power, and not through mere majority preference, but through careful, prayerful discernment that seeks and expects the Spirit's guidance even amid contested questions.

## 10. Equipping Rather Than Performing

Ephesians connects the Spirit's work in the church to the raising up of leaders. Christ gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ.

Notice the purpose. They are not given to accumulate authority for its own sake or to perform ministry on behalf of a passive congregation. They are given to equip ordinary believers for the work of ministry themselves.

:::quote Chapter Eleven
The Spirit's design for the church is not a small number of gifted professionals doing all the meaningful ministry while everyone else observes. It is a body where every member, equipped and released by gifted leaders, actively participates in building up the whole.
:::

This vision protects against two opposite errors: a passive congregation that outsources all spiritual responsibility to paid staff, and a chaotic congregation lacking the leadership and structure the Spirit Himself has provided.

The final glimpse Scripture gives stretches far beyond the first century. Through the church, the manifold wisdom of God is made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places. Every local congregation, however small or unremarkable by worldly measures, participates in this larger, Spirit-empowered mission.

## Reflection

Pay attention tomorrow to what fills the small gaps of your day: waiting in line, driving, the minutes before sleep. These gaps reveal more about which mindset governs you than your Sunday morning intentions do. Replace one habitual gap-filler this week with a short turn toward Scripture or quiet prayer, not as a rigid rule but as a small, repeated act of setting your mind on the things of the Spirit.

## Summary

Walking by the Spirit is a matter of direction rather than suppression, and Paul's ordering in Galatians 5:16 reverses the approach most believers instinctively take. He is honest that a real internal conflict remains after regeneration, and feeling it is normal rather than disqualifying. The Spirit's chief means of change is the renewal of the mind through Scripture, which reaches beneath behaviour into how we think and desire. He leads rather than drives, through promptings that grow clearer as they are obeyed in small things, and He can be grieved through sin or quenched through neglect without ever withdrawing His permanent presence. Turning outward, the same Spirit formed the first church, initiated its mission at Antioch, guided its resolution of genuine conflict at Jerusalem, and gave leaders whose purpose is to equip ordinary believers rather than to perform ministry for them. Every congregation participates in a mission whose significance reaches beyond what we can see.

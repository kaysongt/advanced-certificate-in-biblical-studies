Before we can understand what it means to be saved, we have to understand what we were saved from and, more importantly, what we were saved for. That story does not begin with sin. It begins with a garden, a voice, and a decision to create.

Genesis opens with six words that quietly rearrange everything we think we know about the universe. In the beginning, God created.

:::objectives By the end of this lesson you will be able to
1. Explain what the repeated declaration that creation was *good* rules out about the origin of brokenness.
2. Show from the order and manner of creation that God's purpose was relational.
3. Explain the significance of the shift in language when God creates humanity.
4. Define the image of God and explain what it establishes about human dignity.
5. Explain work, dominion, and community as parts of the original design rather than later additions.
6. Explain what the seventh day anticipates about the logic of grace.
:::

## Key Scriptures

:::scripture Genesis 1:26 to 27
Then God said, "Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness"... So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.
:::

- **Genesis 1:26 to 28** — made in God's image, blessed, and given dominion
- **Genesis 2:1 to 3** — God rests and invites His creation into that rhythm
- **Genesis 2:15** — placed in Eden to tend it and keep it
- **Genesis 2:18, 23** — it is not good for the man to be alone
- **Psalm 8:3 to 6** — what is man that You are mindful of him
- **Psalm 139:13 to 18** — fearfully and wonderfully made
- **Colossians 1:16 to 17** — all things created through Christ and for Christ
- **Acts 17:26 to 28** — in Him we live and move and have our being
- **James 3:9** — people made in the likeness of God
- **Revelation 4:11** — all things were created for His pleasure

## Key Terms

:::terms
Imago Dei | Latin, image of God | The truth that human beings were designed to reflect God, grounding a dignity that is given rather than earned.
Dominion | | The rule God grants humanity over creation, mirroring the way He Himself rules: with care, wisdom, and provision rather than careless extraction.
Stewardship | | Responsible management of what belongs to another. Adam was placed in the garden to tend and keep it.
Sabbath Rest | | The seventh-day pattern in which rest precedes labour, anticipating the logic of grace.
Original Design | | God's intention for creation before the fall, which redemption restores rather than replaces.
:::

## 1. It Was Good

What strikes every reader of Genesis 1 is the rhythm of the chapter. Light. Sky. Land and sea. Sun, moon, and stars. Fish and birds. Land animals. And after each movement of creative power, the same phrase appears like a drumbeat. God saw that it was good. Not neutral. Not merely functional. Good, the way a craftsman looks at finished work and knows it is exactly what he intended.

:::quote Chapter One
If creation began broken, then brokenness would simply be the nature of things, and no one could be blamed for a world gone wrong. But Genesis will not let us off that easily. The universe did not stumble into existence damaged. Whatever went wrong later, went wrong. It was not the plan.
:::

Colossians pushes this further. All things were created through Christ and for Christ, and in Him all things hold together. Creation was never a random experiment. It had a purpose, and that purpose was relational. God did not build a machine and walk away. He built a home, and He built it for fellowship.

## 2. Provision Before Need

Consider the order in which creation unfolds. Before God ever forms a human being, He prepares an environment fit for one. Light before eyes to see it. Land before feet to walk on it. Fruit trees before mouths to enjoy them.

Every provision exists before the need for it does. This is not the behaviour of a distant deity indifferent to His creatures. It is the behaviour of a Father preparing a nursery before His child arrives.

## 3. The Shift in Language

On the sixth day, something changes in the pattern. For every other act of creation, the text simply records what God said and what happened. Let there be light, and there was light. When it comes to humanity, the language shifts into something more intimate. Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness.

There is deliberation here. There is intention. Humanity is not one more item on a list. Humanity is the centrepiece of the entire project.

Psalm 8 captures the wonder. The psalmist looks up at the heavens and feels the weight of his own smallness. What is man, that You are mindful of him? It is a fair question. The psalm answers it with astonishment rather than despair: God crowned humanity with glory and honour and gave them dominion over the works of His hands.

## 4. What the Image Means

At its core, being made in God's image means human beings were designed to reflect Him. Not to be Him. Not to replace Him.

:::quote Chapter Two
To reflect Him the way a mirror reflects light it did not create but was made to display.
:::

This changes the foundation on which human dignity rests. Your worth is not something you earn through performance, and it is not something you can lose through failure. It was given to you the moment God formed you, and it belongs to every human being regardless of nationality, ability, age, or achievement.

James makes a striking argument along these lines when he rebukes believers for cursing people who were made in the likeness of God. Notice that James does not qualify his statement. He does not say some people bear God's image. He says people, all people, bear it.

:::warn Why identity built elsewhere collapses
Achievement fades. Beauty fades. Approval from other people is inconsistent and often cruel. But being made in the image of God is not contingent on any of those things. It was true of you before you accomplished anything, and it remains true of you no matter how far you have fallen.
:::

## 5. Work, Dominion, and Community

Three parts of the design are often mistaken for later additions.

**Work.** God blessed the man and woman and gave them a job before sin ever entered the picture. Be fruitful and multiply, fill the earth, and have dominion over it. Work is not a punishment invented after the fall. Work is part of what it means to bear God's image. We were made to create, to cultivate, to organize, to lead, to build, because the God whose image we carry does all of those things Himself.

**Dominion.** This has sometimes been misunderstood as licence for exploitation rather than an invitation to stewardship. The dominion described in Scripture mirrors the way God Himself rules, with care, wisdom, and provision for what is entrusted to Him. To bear God's image while exercising dominion means ruling the way He rules, cultivating rather than merely consuming, tending rather than merely taking.

**Community.** God looks at Adam, alone in an otherwise finished and good creation, and declares that it is not good for the man to be alone. This is the first time in the creation account that anything is described as *not good*, and the solution is not a task or an achievement but a companion. When Adam sees her for the first time, his response is the first recorded poem in human history, a burst of recognition and delight.

## 6. Personal, Not Abstract

Psalm 139 offers one of the most personal descriptions of human dignity found anywhere in Scripture. David is not writing theology in the abstract. He is writing about himself, about the specific and deliberate way God formed him in his mother's womb.

What is remarkable is the emotional register. David does not describe his creation with clinical detachment. He describes it with wonder, almost disbelief, that a being as small as himself could be the object of such intentional care.

:::quote Chapter Two
This is not the language of an accident being examined. It is the language of a masterpiece being appreciated.
:::

Paul makes the same point standing before the philosophers of Athens, telling them that God made every nation from one blood so that people would seek Him and perhaps feel their way toward Him, though He is not far from any of us. We are not self-sufficient beings who occasionally consider spiritual questions. We are creatures built with God-shaped space at the centre of who we are.

## 7. Rest Before Labour

Genesis describes God resting on the seventh day, not because He was tired, but because the work was complete and He was inviting His creation into that same rhythm.

Rest, in the biblical pattern, comes *before* labour rather than after it. Adam's very first full day of existence was a day of rest with God, not a day of striving to prove himself worthy of a place in the garden.

:::quote Chapter One
This detail quietly anticipates the entire logic of grace that will unfold throughout the rest of Scripture. We are invited to rest in what God has provided before we are ever asked to work. Grace comes first. Labor is the response, never the entry fee.
:::

Even the two trees at the centre of the garden reveal something. God did not withhold the tree of life. It stood freely available, a continual offer of ongoing fellowship. The single restriction concerned only one tree, and even that came wrapped in explanation rather than arbitrary demand. This is the design of a generous Father who gave freedom within loving boundaries.

## 8. What This Sets Up

Every doctrine in the coming lessons, sin, grace, faith, justification, regeneration, sanctification, glorification, only makes sense in light of this starting point. God made a good world and good people for the purpose of fellowship with Himself.

:::quote Chapter Two
Before you were ever told you were a sinner in need of a Savior, the Bible tells you that you were made by a God who delighted in making you.
:::

Salvation does not give you a dignity you never had. It restores a dignity that sin corrupted. The image of God in every human being is like a mirror that has been cracked. It still reflects something of the one who made it, but the reflection is distorted, fragmented, and in need of restoration.

## Discussion Questions

1. Why does it matter theologically that creation was declared good rather than neutral? What would follow if brokenness were simply the nature of things?
2. Trace the ways provision precedes need in Genesis 1. What does that pattern say about God's character, and where do you fail to trust it?
3. Explain the image of God to someone who ties their worth to productivity. What specifically does the doctrine dismantle in their reasoning?
4. Work, dominion, and community all predate the fall. Which of the three does your church culture most often treat as a consequence of sin rather than part of the design?
5. Rest precedes labour in the creation account. Where in your own spiritual life have you reversed that order?

## Summary

Salvation cannot be understood without the garden. Genesis declares creation good repeatedly, which rules out treating brokenness as simply the nature of things. God prepared every provision before the need existed, built a home rather than a machine, and shifted from command to deliberation when He made humanity in His own image. That image grounds a dignity given rather than earned and belonging to every person without qualification. Work, dominion exercised as stewardship, and human community all belong to the original design rather than arriving as consequences of sin. And the seventh day sets the pattern the rest of Scripture will follow: rest comes before labour, grace before response. Redemption is not a second plan. It is the restoration of this one. Lesson 2 examines how the mirror cracked.

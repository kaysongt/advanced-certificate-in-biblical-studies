This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 104 and the whole of *What We Believe, Volume 4: Salvation*. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: **80%**.

## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q Why does the textbook begin with creation rather than sin?
+ Redemption restores what God always intended, so the cross cannot be understood without the garden
- Because Genesis is the first book of the Bible
- Because the doctrine of sin is too discouraging to open with
- Because creation is less theologically contested than the fall
= Before we can understand what it means to be saved, we have to understand what we were saved from and what we were saved for.

Q What does being made in the image of God establish?
+ A dignity that is given rather than earned, and cannot be lost through failure
- That humanity shares God's divine essence
- That some human beings reflect God more fully than others
- That physical appearance carries theological significance
= James rebukes believers for cursing people made in God's likeness without qualifying which people bear it.

Q What does the seventh day of creation anticipate?
+ The logic of grace, since rest precedes labour rather than following it
- The institution of the Sabbath as a legal requirement
- God's need for recovery after creative exertion
- The completion of the covenant with Adam
= Adam's first full day of existence was a day of rest with God, not a day of striving to prove himself worthy of a place in the garden.

Q What strategy did the serpent employ in Genesis 3?
+ Reframing abundant generosity as restrictive stinginess, planting doubt about God's character
- Directly commanding disobedience to God's word
- Denying that God existed at all
- Promising material wealth in exchange for allegiance
= Temptation rarely begins with an outright command to sin. It begins with a subtle reinterpretation of who God is.

Q What do the fig leaves represent?
+ The first religious act in human history, and a failure, since human effort cannot cover guilt
- An appropriate expression of newfound modesty
- A symbol of the coming sacrificial system
- Evidence that Adam and Eve had not fully understood their sin
= Human effort to fix what sin broke never quite works, a pattern repeating until Christ accomplishes what no human effort could.

Q How does Genesis 3:21 preview the gospel?
+ Only a covering God provides, at a cost He bears, actually suffices
- It establishes the priesthood as mediator between God and man
- It shows that clothing has symbolic value in worship
- It demonstrates God's concern for physical comfort
= God made tunics of skin, requiring the first blood shed in Scripture, establishing the pattern every later sacrifice would follow.

Q How does the textbook define sin?
+ A condition, a fractured relationship with God that produces the behaviours we notice first
- A catalogue of prohibited actions
- A weakness of will that discipline can overcome
- The corrupting influence of society on individuals
= The Bible describes sin as missing a target, as rebellion, and as debt. Together these describe a condition rather than merely a set of acts.

Q Why does the doctrine of sin level every ground of comparison?
+ Because the problem is a matter of the heart, which cannot be ranked by external behaviour
- Because all sins receive identical consequences in this life
- Because God is indifferent to degrees of wrongdoing
- Because the law applied only to Israel
= The gospel is not a message reserved for people who have sinned less. It is a message for everyone.

Q What does grace claim?
+ God helps those who cannot help themselves at all
- God helps those who help themselves
- God rewards sincere effort with additional assistance
- God reduces the penalty sin deserves
= It is not a discount on a bill we still owe. It is the bill paid in full by Someone else, without our contribution.

Q According to Titus 2, what does grace do besides save?
+ It teaches, training believers to deny ungodliness and live godly lives
- It guarantees material prosperity to the faithful
- It removes the possibility of future sin
- It suspends the moral requirements of the law
= Grace is not a licence to live however we please. It produces a changed life as a response to a gift too great to leave us unchanged.

Q What was the function of the law?
+ A mirror revealing the heart's condition and a guardian until Christ came, unable to cure what it diagnosed
- A ladder by which the righteous could ascend to God
- A temporary code now abolished and irrelevant
- The means of salvation for Israel before the cross
= The law could diagnose the disease of sin with perfect accuracy, but it had no power to cure it.

Q What is the significance of *accounted* in Romans 4:3?
+ An accounting term describing something credited that did not originate from the person's own effort
- A term indicating God's approval of Abraham's obedience
- A description of gradual moral improvement over time
- A provisional status pending final assessment
= Righteousness was credited to Abraham's account the moment he believed, long before his obedience caught up with his faith.

Q How does the textbook reconcile Paul and James on faith and works?
+ Paul defends that we are justified by faith alone; James defends that the faith which justifies is never alone
- Paul and James address different covenants
- James corrects an error in Paul's teaching
- Paul addresses initial salvation and James final judgment
= The difference is not disagreement about how salvation works. It is a difference in emphasis.

Q What does *metanoia* literally mean?
+ A change of mind
- A turning back
- A godly sorrow
- A confession of guilt
= It describes a fundamental reorientation of thinking that produces a corresponding change in direction and behaviour.

Q What distinguishes godly sorrow from worldly sorrow?
+ Godly sorrow grieves the offence against God and produces lasting change; worldly sorrow grieves consequences and fades
- Godly sorrow is more intense emotionally
- Worldly sorrow is felt by unbelievers only
- Godly sorrow is expressed publicly
= Worldly sorrow can be overwhelming yet produces nothing lasting, because its focus remains entirely on the self and its losses.

Q What does justification mean?
+ A legal declaration of righteousness rendered once and completely on the basis of Christ's finished work
- A gradual process of being made righteous
- The overlooking of sin without a penalty being paid
- A provisional status confirmed at the final judgment
= Justification is not about becoming. It is about being declared, once and finally.

Q How can God justify the ungodly without corrupting justice?
+ Because the demands of justice were fully satisfied at the cross before the verdict was rendered
- Because divine mercy overrides divine justice
- Because human categories of justice do not apply to God
- Because guilt was transferred to the accuser
= This is why Paul can describe God as both just and the justifier in the same breath. It is not a loophole but the most costly verdict in history.

Q What is the difference between regeneration and reformation?
+ Reformation asks a person to try harder with the same heart; regeneration gives a new heart inclined toward God
- Reformation is instantaneous while regeneration is gradual
- Reformation concerns belief while regeneration concerns behaviour
- There is no meaningful difference between them
= Reformation operates within the nature a person already possessed, polishing the surface without altering what lies beneath.

Q What does Roman adoption practice contribute to Paul's theology?
+ Adoption erased old identity and debts, granting full heir status equal to natural-born children
- Adopted children received a lesser share of the inheritance
- Adoption was provisional and revocable
- Adoption required the consent of the extended family
= This is precisely the picture Paul intends by calling believers joint heirs with Christ.

Q What distinguishes the objective from the subjective ground of assurance?
+ The objective is Christ's finished work, unchanging; the subjective is the Spirit's witness and observable fruit, which fluctuate
- The objective is doctrinal and the subjective is emotional in a way that makes it worthless
- The objective applies to the mature and the subjective to new believers
- They are two names for the same reality
= Mature assurance rests its full weight on the objective ground while welcoming the subjective as confirmation.
:::

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

**B1.** Explain why the textbook begins with creation rather than sin, and what is lost when a gospel presentation skips the garden entirely.

**B2.** Explain representative headship as Paul uses it in Romans 5. Show how the structure that seems unjust in Adam becomes the ground of hope in Christ.

**B3.** Define grace and faith, and explain the relationship between them using the image of the gift and the empty hand. Then explain why boasting is excluded.

**B4.** Explain how God can declare a guilty person righteous without becoming a corrupt judge. Use Romans 3 and define propitiation in your answer.

**B5.** Distinguish justification from sanctification. Then explain how the security of the former makes genuine pursuit of the latter possible rather than undermining it.

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> Dr. Kay observes that many sincere believers intellectually agree with justification by faith while continuing to live as though their standing with God rises and falls with their daily performance. Explain what has gone wrong in that believer's understanding, using at least four passages from this course. Then describe, concretely, how you would teach or counsel them out of it, including what you would expect to change in their prayer life, their response to failure, and their motivation for obedience.

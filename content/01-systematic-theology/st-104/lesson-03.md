If sin is humanity reaching for something it had no right to take, grace is God reaching down to give us something we could never earn.

Every other religious system in human history operates on a transaction. Perform well enough, sacrifice enough, meditate enough, and perhaps the divine will notice and reward you. Christianity begins somewhere entirely different. It begins with a Giver who moves first, before we have done anything to deserve His attention.

:::objectives By the end of this lesson you will be able to
1. Define grace precisely and explain why the word *freely* in Romans 3:24 excludes partial earning.
2. Explain Ephesians 2:8 to 9 and why every component of salvation originates outside the believer.
3. Explain how Titus 2:11 to 14 corrects the misuse of grace as licence.
4. Explain the relationship between law and grace.
5. Define faith and distinguish it from two common misunderstandings.
6. Explain the significance of the word *accounted* in Romans 4 and reconcile Paul with James.
7. Explain why the object of faith rather than its intensity determines the outcome.
:::

## Key Scriptures

:::scripture Ephesians 2:8 to 9

:::

- **John 1:16 to 17** — grace and truth came through Jesus Christ
- **Romans 3:24** — justified freely by His grace
- **Ephesians 2:8 to 9** — the gift of God, not of works
- **Titus 2:11 to 14** — grace teaches us to deny ungodliness
- **1 Corinthians 15:10** — by the grace of God I am what I am

## Key Terms

:::terms
Grace | Greek *charis* | Unearned favour. Not a discount on a bill we still owe, but the bill paid in full by Someone else, without our contribution.
Faith | Greek *pistis* | Confident trust grounded in the reliability of the One being trusted, expressed in a willingness to act on what God has said.
Imputation | | The crediting of something to a person's account that did not originate from their own effort. Romans 4 uses the accounting term *accounted*.
Sola Gratia | Latin, grace alone | The conviction that salvation originates entirely with God's initiative rather than any human contribution.
Common Ground of Paul and James | | Paul defends that we are justified by faith alone; James defends that the faith which justifies is never alone.
:::

## 1. What Grace Actually Claims

The word grace, in its simplest definition, means unearned favour. That short definition does not capture the scandal of what grace actually claims.

:::quote Chapter Five
Grace does not say God will help those who help themselves. Grace says God helps those who cannot help themselves at all. It is not a discount on a bill we still owe. It is the bill paid in full by Someone else, on our behalf, without our contribution.
:::

John draws the contrast at the opening of his Gospel. The law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ. The law revealed God's standard, a standard no one could keep perfectly. Grace and truth revealed something the law never could, a God who gives rather than merely demands.

Paul develops the theme in Romans, describing believers as justified **freely** by His grace. That word is not a minor detail. It means grace cannot be purchased, negotiated, or partially earned through effort.

## 2. Nothing Left to Boast About

Perhaps no passage states the nature of grace more clearly than Ephesians 2:8 and 9. Notice the careful construction. Salvation is by grace. It comes through faith. And Paul goes out of his way to specify that even this is not of yourselves.

Some readers wonder whether that phrase refers to grace, to faith, or to salvation as a whole. The theological point remains the same regardless. Every component of your rescue originated outside of you.

:::quote Chapter Five
Our only contribution to our salvation was the sin that made it necessary. Everything else belongs to Him.
:::

This is precisely why grace offends human pride even as it delights the human heart. We tend to want some credit, however small, for our own rescue. Grace refuses to give us any.

Dr. Kay puts the distinction memorably. Grace is the difference between a rescue and a reward. A reward is given because someone did something to deserve it. A rescue is given because someone was in danger and could not save themselves. Grace is God diving into the water to pull us out, not applauding from the shore while we struggle to swim to safety.

## 3. Grace as Teacher

Titus broadens the picture and corrects a common misunderstanding. The grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people, and this grace *teaches* us to deny ungodliness and worldly lusts, and to live soberly, righteously, and godly in the present age.

:::warn Grace is not licence
Grace is not permission to live however we please because forgiveness is guaranteed. Grace is a teacher. It trains us, shapes us, and produces a changed life, not because we are trying to earn favour we already possess, but because we are responding to a gift too great to leave unchanged by it.
:::

## 4. Law and Grace

It helps to see grace against the backdrop of the law it fulfills rather than replaces.

The law given through Moses was never intended to be a ladder by which people could climb to God through their own righteousness. It functioned more like a mirror, revealing the true condition of the human heart, and like a guardian, watching over humanity until the fullness of time.

The law could diagnose the disease of sin with perfect accuracy, but it had no power to cure it. It could tell people exactly what righteousness looked like without ever being able to produce that righteousness within them.

:::quote Chapter Five
Grace does what the law never could. It does not lower the standard of God's holiness. It meets that standard fully in Christ and then credits the result to everyone who believes.
:::

Paul lived his entire ministry from this posture. By the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace toward me was not in vain, but I laboured more abundantly than they all, yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me (1 Corinthians 15:10). Even Paul's tireless labour, he insists, was grace working through him.

Grace is not simply the beginning of your salvation story. It is the entire atmosphere in which that story takes place. There is no point along the way where grace hands the baton to your own performance.

## 5. Two Mistakes About Faith

Grace explains what God has done. Faith explains how a person receives it. Two common mistakes miss what the Bible means by the word.

| Mistake | What it does |
|---|---|
| Faith as a work | Treats faith as a spiritual accomplishment performed to earn God's favour, one more rung on the ladder of human effort |
| Faith as mere assent | Treats faith as intellectual agreement with facts about Jesus, without accompanying trust or transformation |

Hebrews 11:1 offers the closest thing to a formal definition. Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. Faith is not blind optimism or wishful thinking. It is confident trust grounded in the reliability of the One being trusted.

The writer illustrates this through Old Testament believers, and the pattern is instructive. Abel offered a sacrifice by faith. Noah built an ark by faith before he had ever seen a drop of rain. Abraham left his homeland by faith, not knowing where he was going. In every case, faith is a concrete response to God's specific word, a willingness to act on what God has said before the evidence of its fulfillment becomes visible.

Hebrews 11:6 draws a sobering conclusion. Without faith it is impossible to please Him. Faith is not one spiritual discipline among many that a mature believer might eventually develop. It is the entry point.

## 6. Accounted

Paul develops the doctrine most fully in Romans 4, using Abraham as his primary example. The choice was strategic, because Abraham was revered by Jewish readers as the father of their nation. Paul's argument is that Abraham was not justified by circumcision, by obedience to the law, or by any work at all, but by faith, and that this happened before he was ever circumcised.

Romans 4:3 quotes Genesis 15:6 directly. Abraham believed God, and it was **accounted** to him for righteousness.

:::quote Chapter Six
That word accounted carries important weight. It is an accounting term, describing something credited to a person's record that did not originate from their own effort. Righteousness was credited to his account the moment he believed God's promise, long before his obedience ever caught up with his faith.
:::

Romans 10:17 explains how faith actually develops. Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Faith is not something we manufacture out of sheer willpower. It is a response produced by exposure to God's word. This is why consistent teaching and reading of Scripture matters so deeply.

## 7. Paul and James

Some readers sense a tension between Paul's teaching on faith and James's teaching that faith without works is dead.

James is not contradicting Paul, and he is not describing a different kind of faith that adds works as a supplementary requirement for salvation. James is describing what genuine faith inevitably produces. A living faith will always express itself through corresponding action, because trust that never affects behaviour is not the trust the Bible is describing at all.

Paul would agree entirely, since Paul himself insists that believers are created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand.

:::note The clean formulation
The difference is not a disagreement about how salvation works. It is a difference in emphasis. Paul defends the truth that we are justified by faith alone. James defends the truth that the faith that justifies is never alone.
:::

## 8. The Object, Not the Intensity

Many believers carry quiet anxiety about whether their faith is strong enough or sincere enough.

The good news of the gospel is not that your faith must be perfect or unshakable. The good news is that faith, however small, connects you to a Savior whose work is perfect and complete. Jesus told His disciples that faith the size of a mustard seed could move mountains, not because the faith itself is magical, but because even the smallest genuine trust in an infinitely powerful God is enough.

:::quote Chapter Six
Two people can sit in the same chair with very different levels of confidence, one gripping the armrests nervously and another sitting back completely relaxed, yet the chair holds them both equally well, because the chair's strength, not their feelings about it, determines the outcome.
:::

It is not the size of your faith that saves you. It is the sufficiency of the One your faith, however small, is resting upon.

This is the essential difference between grace and faith. Grace is the gift. Faith is the empty hand that receives it. An empty hand does not earn the gift it receives. It simply opens to accept what is being offered. Faith does not save you because of some inherent power within faith itself. Faith saves you because it connects you to the One who does the saving.

## Discussion Questions

1. Explain grace to someone who believes God helps those who help themselves. Where exactly does their formulation break down?
2. Titus says grace teaches. How would you answer someone who argues that emphasizing grace will make people careless about holiness?
3. Explain the function of the law using the images of mirror and guardian. What goes wrong when the law is treated as a ladder instead?
4. Both mistakes about faith, faith-as-work and faith-as-assent, are common. Which is more prevalent in your context, and what does it produce?
5. Reconcile Paul and James in a single sentence, then explain the sentence to someone who has been told the Bible contradicts itself here.

## Summary

Grace means God helps those who cannot help themselves, a bill paid in full rather than a discount on one still owed. Romans and Ephesians close off every route to boasting, since every component of rescue originated outside us and our only contribution was the sin that made it necessary. Grace is nevertheless a teacher rather than a licence, training those it saves. It does not lower God's standard but meets it in Christ and credits the result to those who believe. Faith is neither a work nor bare intellectual assent but confident trust that acts on God's word, and Romans 4 shows righteousness credited to Abraham's account before his obedience caught up. Paul and James agree: justification is by faith alone, and the faith that justifies is never alone. And what matters is not the intensity of your grip but the strength of what holds you. Lesson 4 turns to the response faith produces and the verdict it receives.

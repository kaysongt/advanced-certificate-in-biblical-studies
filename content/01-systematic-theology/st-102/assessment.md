## Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

:::quiz Section A · 40 points
Q Where does the Bible begin the story of Jesus?
+ In eternity, before creation, with the Word who was already there when the beginning began
- At the annunciation to Mary
- At His birth in Bethlehem
- At His baptism, when He was declared the Son of God
= John's opening echoes Genesis on purpose. He is not introducing a new character but identifying someone already present before anything was made.

Q What does *firstborn over all creation* mean in Colossians 1:15?
+ Preeminence and rank
- Chronological priority as the first created being
- Priority in resurrection over all who would follow
- Legal inheritance rights within the house of David
= Paul settles it in the next verses: He is before all things, and in Him all things consist. You cannot be before all things and also be one of the things made.

Q What does Hebrews mean by saying Christ is *upholding* all things?
+ He actively sustains the universe at this moment by His word of power
- He established the laws of nature at creation and left them to operate
- He preserves the moral law among the nations
- He will sustain creation only until the day of judgment
= Nothing in your life is happening outside His sustaining care. The power that keeps planets in orbit is at work in the details of your ordinary days.

Q Which two truths does the virgin birth protect?
+ Christ's genuine humanity, and His deity together with His sinlessness
- Mary's righteousness, and Joseph's legal paternity
- The reliability of the genealogies, and the fulfillment of Micah
- His descent from David, and His freedom from Roman citizenship
= He was genuinely born of a woman, and His conception bypassed the transmission of a fallen human nature through a human father.

Q What did Christ empty Himself of in the *kenosis*?
+ The independent exercise of divine privilege and His visible glory
- His divine nature, resumed at the resurrection
- His moral perfection, so that He could be genuinely tempted
- Nothing, since the passage is purely poetic
= He remained fully God throughout His earthly life, choosing to live in dependence on the Father and submission to the Spirit.

Q Why does Paul specify that the Son was born *under the law*?
+ To redeem those under the law's curse He had to fulfill it from within their condition rather than from exemption
- To establish His right to teach in the synagogues
- To show that Gentiles are excluded from redemption
- To demonstrate that the law was never intended to be kept
= His fulfillment of every requirement they had failed to meet could then be credited to their account.

Q Which detail from the Gospels most directly establishes Christ's genuine human development?
+ Luke's statement that He increased in wisdom and stature
- His hunger after fasting in the wilderness
- His weariness at the well in Samaria
- His need for sleep during the storm
= All four show His humanity, but *increased* specifically establishes genuine developmental growth over time rather than momentary physical need.

Q How did Jesus resist temptation in the wilderness?
+ By standing on the authority of Scripture, answering from Deuteronomy
- By exercising divine power unavailable to ordinary believers
- By invoking angelic assistance
- By extending His fast until the temptations ceased
= His victory models the pattern of resistance available to every believer who hides God's word in their heart.

Q Why would a single sin have disqualified Christ?
+ He would have needed a Savior of His own and could not have substituted for anyone else
- The Jewish authorities would have had grounds to reject Him
- The law required ritual purity in all who taught publicly
- His miracles would have ceased
= Romans 5:19 sets out the logic. By one Man's obedience many will be made righteous, which requires that obedience to be complete.

Q Which is the clearest single test of deity applied in Chapter 4?
+ The reception of worship without correction
- The performance of miracles
- The claim to forgive sins
- The fulfillment of messianic prophecy
= Angels refuse worship and faithful men refuse worship. Jesus received it repeatedly and never once corrected those who offered it.

Q Which statement correctly expresses the doctrine of the two natures?
+ One Person, two complete and undiminished natures, united without confusion and without separation
- Two Persons in one body, cooperating perfectly
- One Person whose two natures blended into a third kind of nature
- One Person alternating between divine and human modes of action
= This is why the Gospels describe Him sleeping in a boat and calming the storm within the same narrative.

Q If Christ were Priest and King but not Prophet, what would be missing?
+ A clear revelation of who God actually is
- Any way to have our sin dealt with
- Ultimate authority over the forces of evil
- The fulfillment of the Melchizedek order
= He reveals the Father as Prophet, reconciles us as Priest, and rules and protects us as King. The completeness of His ministry mirrors the completeness of our need.

Q What does the title *Christ* mean?
+ The Anointed One
- The Son of God
- The Savior
- The Deliverer
= It is a title rather than a surname, the Greek equivalent of the Hebrew Messiah, marking installation into prophetic, priestly, and kingly office.

Q Why does John call the miracles *signs*?
+ The word points beyond the event to what it reveals about His identity
- The miracles were symbolic rather than physically real
- They served as public proof for legal witnesses
- They signalled the beginning of each phase of His ministry
= John states his purpose: these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God.

Q What does *propitiation* mean?
+ The full satisfaction of God's righteous wrath against sin through Christ's sacrifice
- The covering over of sin so that it is no longer visible
- The restoration of a broken relationship
- The legal declaration that a sinner stands righteous
= Sin against an infinitely holy God demands a response, and at the cross that response fell on Christ instead of on us.

Q What is significant about the veil being torn from top to bottom?
+ God Himself removed the barrier from His side
- The tearing was caused by the earthquake rather than by design
- It indicated the destruction of the temple to come
- It signalled the end of the priestly office entirely
= A veil torn from the bottom might suggest human effort breaking through from outside. Hebrews describes a new and living way opened for us through His flesh.

Q Which detail in the resurrection accounts argues most strongly against fabrication?
+ Women were the first witnesses, in a culture where their testimony carried little weight
- The Roman guard placed at the tomb
- The forty-day period of appearances
- The variation among the four Gospel accounts
= If invented to persuade a sceptical audience, this is precisely the detail a fabricator would have changed.

Q What does calling Christ the *firstfruits* establish?
+ His resurrection guarantees that all united to Him by faith will likewise be raised
- His was the first resurrection recorded in Scripture
- His resurrection completed the Old Testament feast cycle
- His resurrection was the first of several in the apostolic period
= The firstfruits were the first portion of a harvest, guaranteeing the rest would follow in due course.

Q What is Christ doing now, at the Father's right hand?
+ Interceding and advocating for His people, shepherding the church, sending the Spirit, and exercising all authority
- Waiting passively until the appointed time of His return
- Preparing dwelling places while the Spirit governs the church independently
- Reigning over heaven while earthly affairs proceed under delegated powers
= Romans 8:34 gives the progression: Christ died, rose, ascended, and right now makes intercession for us.

Q According to Acts 1:11, how will Christ return?
+ Personally, visibly, and as certainly as the ascension the disciples had just witnessed
- Secretly, so that only believers perceive His coming
- Spiritually, through the growth of the church in the world
- Progressively, as His Kingdom advances among the nations
= This same Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will so come in like manner as you saw Him go.
:::

## Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each, 30 points total. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

**B1.** Explain why the preexistence of Christ is necessary for salvation. Build the argument in steps rather than asserting the conclusion.

**B2.** Define *kenosis* precisely. State what Christ set aside and what He retained, and explain how Philippians 2:5 turns the doctrine into an instruction for believers.

**B3.** Establish the deity of Christ using one passage from each of the four categories of evidence: names, attributes, works, and worship received.

**B4.** Explain what would be missing from our salvation if Christ held only two of His three offices. Work through all three possible omissions.

**B5.** Define atonement, reconciliation, justification, the great exchange, and victory. Then explain why the resurrection is necessary for any of them to stand.

:::note Marking guide for Section B
Full marks require an accurate definition, at least one correctly cited passage, and evidence of understanding why the point matters. Reproducing textbook wording without demonstrating comprehension receives at most half marks.
:::

## Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

> Dr. Kay writes that a wrong view of Jesus produces a wrong gospel, and a wrong gospel cannot save anyone. Choose one doctrine from this course, either the full deity of Christ, His full humanity, or the bodily resurrection. Show from at least three passages how Scripture establishes it, explain precisely what would collapse in the gospel if that doctrine were surrendered, and describe how you would teach it to a congregation that has never been given reasons for believing it.

| Rigour of the argument connecting the doctrine to the gospel's coherence | 10 |

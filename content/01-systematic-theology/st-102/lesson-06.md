Part Three of the textbook covers the exalted Christ, and it takes up three chapters that popular Christian teaching tends to skip.

The ascension is often overshadowed by the crucifixion and resurrection. The present ministry of Christ is, in Dr. Kay's words, one of the most overlooked truths in popular Christian teaching. And the second coming is more often speculated about than understood.

Taken together they answer a question the previous lessons leave open. If Christ rose, where is He now, and what is He doing?

:::objectives By the end of this lesson you will be able to
1. Describe the ascension and explain why it marks a promotion rather than a departure.
2. Explain the significance of Christ ascending bodily and retaining His human nature permanently.
3. Describe Christ's present ministry under the headings of intercession, advocacy, shepherding, sending the Spirit, and exercising authority.
4. Explain how Christ's present ministry reshapes prayer, assurance, and spiritual formation.
5. State the character of Christ's return as personal, visible, and certain.
6. Explain what His return will accomplish and describe the response Scripture calls for.
:::

## Key Terms

:::terms
Ascension | | Christ's bodily return to the Father's presence forty days after the resurrection, marking the completion of His earthly ministry and the beginning of His exalted reign.
Session | Latin *sessio*, a sitting | Christ's being seated at the Father's right hand, the position of highest honour and delegated authority.
Intercession | | Christ's continuous ministry of representing His people before the Father, applying the merit of His finished sacrifice to their ongoing need.
Advocate | Greek *parakletos* | One who represents another and speaks on their behalf, particularly in a legal or accusatory setting.
Parousia | Greek, arrival or presence | The technical term for Christ's second coming.
Blessed Hope | | Paul's description in Titus 2:13 of the believer's anticipation of Christ's appearing, framed as joy rather than dread.
:::

## 1. The Ascension

Forty days after His resurrection, having appeared repeatedly to His disciples and instructed them concerning the Kingdom of God, Jesus led His followers out to the Mount of Olives and, as they watched, He was taken up before their eyes, a cloud receiving Him out of their sight. Two angelic messengers appeared and gently redirected the disciples' upward gaze, promising that this same Jesus would return in the same way they had watched Him go (Acts 1:9 to 11).

The detail of the cloud carries deliberate weight for anyone familiar with the Old Testament. Throughout Israel's history a cloud frequently signified the manifest presence and glory of God, from the pillar of cloud that led Israel through the wilderness to the cloud that filled the tabernacle and later the temple. The imagery would have been unmistakable to a first-century Jewish reader. The Son was returning into the very glory of God from which He had come, the glory He had asked to be restored to in John 17:5.

## 2. Enthroned at the Father's Right Hand

Paul describes the enthronement in the language of royal courts. He writes of the power God worked in Christ when He raised Him from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality and power and might and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this age but also in that which is to come (Ephesians 1:20 to 21).

To sit at the right hand of a king in the ancient world was to occupy the position of highest honour and delegated authority.

Paul continues a few verses later, describing how God put all things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all things to the church, which is His body (Ephesians 1:22 to 23). Notice the connection. Christ's cosmic authority is not exercised in isolation from His people. He is head over all things specifically for the sake of the church.

:::quote Chapter Nine
His victory is not distant from us. It is exercised on our behalf.
:::

Hebrews frames the same event in priestly terms, describing Jesus as a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens (Hebrews 4:14). Under the old covenant the high priest entered the earthly tabernacle's Most Holy Place once a year, carrying the blood of sacrifice into a symbolic representation of God's presence. Christ did not enter a symbolic sanctuary made by human hands. He entered heaven itself, carrying not the blood of animals but the merit of His own sacrifice.

## 3. Still Fully Human in Glory

One detail deserves particular emphasis because it is so often overlooked. When Jesus ascended, He did not shed His humanity the way one might set aside a garment no longer needed. He ascended bodily, in the same resurrected, glorified body His disciples had touched and eaten with over the preceding forty days.

:::quote Chapter Nine
This means that at this very moment, seated at the Father's right hand, there exists a glorified human body, the body of Jesus of Nazareth, permanently united to the eternal Son of God. The incarnation was not a temporary arrangement, entered into for thirty three years and then discarded once its purpose was served. It was a permanent union.
:::

Your Savior did not simply visit humanity for a season. He has joined Himself to humanity everlastingly, carrying our nature into the very presence of God, where it remains as a permanent guarantee that the union between God and man, broken at the fall, has been genuinely and lastingly restored.

Luke's Gospel closes with a detail that might surprise a casual reader. After watching Jesus ascend, the disciples did not return to Jerusalem in grief. They worshipped Him and returned with great joy, spending their time continually in the temple, praising and blessing God (Luke 24:52 to 53). That response makes sense only if they understood, even then, that the ascension was a promotion rather than a loss.

## 4. He Intercedes and He Advocates

Christ is not merely a figure from the past whose work we remember, nor a distant king who will return one day. He is actively, continuously ministering on behalf of His people at this very moment.

**Intercession.** Paul asks a rhetorical question in Romans that carries enormous comfort. Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died, and furthermore is also risen, who is even at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us (Romans 8:34). Notice the progression. Christ died. Christ rose. Christ ascended. And right now, at this moment, Christ intercedes.

Hebrews makes an even stronger claim, describing His priesthood as permanent because He continues forever, which means He is able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them (Hebrews 7:24 to 25).

:::quote Chapter Ten
Whatever accusation the enemy of your soul might raise against you, whatever failure or sin threatens to convince you that you have finally exhausted God's patience, Christ is at this moment interceding on your behalf, applying the merit of His finished sacrifice to your ongoing need.
:::

**Advocacy.** John describes a related but distinct aspect, writing to believers who have stumbled. My little children, these things I write to you, so that you may not sin. And if anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous (1 John 2:1 to 2).

An advocate is someone who represents you and speaks on your behalf, particularly in a legal or accusatory context. When you sin as a believer, you do not need to reestablish your standing with God through renewed effort or fresh sacrifice. Christ, who already made the necessary sacrifice once for all, stands as your advocate.

## 5. He Shepherds, He Sends, He Reigns

**He shepherds His church.** He is the head of the body, actively directing and sustaining His people through the ministry of His Word and Spirit. He walks among the churches, as the opening chapters of Revelation vividly picture, aware of their struggles, their faithfulness, and their failures, addressing each with both commendation and correction. This is not the portrait of an absent king ruling from a distance through delegated authority alone.

**He sends and empowers the Spirit.** This is the fulfillment of His own promise that He would ask the Father, and the Father would give another Helper to be with them forever (John 14:16). He had told His disciples it was to their advantage that He go away, so that the Helper could come (John 16:7). The Spirit's ongoing work, convicting of sin, comforting in trial, empowering for ministry, and conforming believers to Christ's image, is itself an expression of Christ's present ministry.

**He exercises all authority.** Before His ascension Jesus told His disciples, all authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth (Matthew 28:18). That authority did not expire when He returned to heaven. Nothing that happens in your life or in the wider world falls outside the reach of His current, active authority.

## 6. What This Changes

**Prayer.** Prayer is not a solitary attempt to get God's attention across an impersonal distance. It is participation in a conversation already being carried forward on your behalf by your interceding High Priest. When words fail you, Christ Himself is already presenting your need before the Father with perfect wisdom and unwavering love.

:::note This does not make your prayer unnecessary
Scripture consistently calls believers to pray earnestly and persistently. What it relieves is the anxious pressure to perform correctly, since the effectiveness of your prayer does not rest on the eloquence of your words but on the perfect intercession of the One who carries them into the Father's presence.
:::

**Assurance.** Jesus said of those who belong to Him, and I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish, neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand (John 10:28). This is not a promise dependent on your strength to hold on. It is grounded in His strength to hold you. The believer's security does not rest on the fragile grip of human resolve.

**Formation.** Christ's present ministry is not merely defensive. Paul describes believers beholding the glory of the Lord and being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord (2 Corinthians 3:18). You are not left to transform yourself through sheer determination, measuring your progress against an impossible standard and despairing at every setback.

**Boldness.** Hebrews connects it all: since we have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need (Hebrews 4:14, 16). You do not approach God on the strength of your own performance, hoping you have done enough to earn a hearing.

## 7. The Promise of His Return

The story does not end with the ascension or the present ministry. It moves toward its conclusion with His promised return.

The angels on the Mount of Olives did not simply tell the disciples that Jesus had gone to heaven. They made a specific promise. This same Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will so come in like manner as you saw Him go (Acts 1:11).

Three features of that promise deserve naming.

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Personal** | It is Jesus Himself who returns, not merely His influence or His Spirit in some abstract sense |
| **Visible** | In the same manner His ascension was visible to those watching |
| **Certain** | As certain as the ascension these disciples had just witnessed with their own eyes |

Matthew records Jesus describing His return in unmistakable terms. Then the sign of the Son of Man will appear in heaven, and then all the tribes of the earth will mourn, and they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory (Matthew 24:30). Paul adds that Christ will transform our lowly body that it may be conformed to His glorious body (Philippians 3:20 to 21).

## 8. A Blessed Hope, Not a Fearful One

Paul instructs Titus that believers are to live godly lives in the present age, looking for the blessed hope and glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ (Titus 2:13).

Notice the phrase *blessed hope*. For the believer, the return of Christ is not a source of dread but of joyful anticipation, the moment when every wrong will finally be set right. This does not mean it carries no note of seriousness. It will indeed be an occasion of judgment for those who have rejected Him. But for those who belong to Him, the New Testament describes it with language of hope and expectation rather than fear.

**What His return will accomplish.**

1. It will **consummate God's Kingdom**, bringing to completion what He announced and inaugurated during His earthly ministry.
2. It will **judge evil**, dealing finally with sin, rebellion, and every power opposed to God's rule. Revelation pictures Him returning as a rider on a white horse, His name called Faithful and True (Revelation 19:11 to 16). This is the same Jesus who was born in a manger and died on a cross, returning now not as a suffering servant but as a conquering King.
3. It will **vindicate His people**, publicly affirming those who trusted Him through difficulty, persecution, and delay.
4. It will **establish everlasting righteousness**, ushering in the new heavens and new earth where righteousness dwells permanently.

## 9. Living With Expectancy

:::warn The wrong response
The proper response is not idle speculation about dates and timelines, a temptation many believers throughout church history have fallen into despite Christ's own clear statement that no one knows the day or the hour.
:::

The proper response is a life shaped by expectancy, holiness, and hope. If Christ could return at any moment, then every day becomes an opportunity to live in a manner worthy of His appearing, not out of anxious fear but out of loving anticipation.

Paul addresses one of the tenderest concerns believers carry about the return, writing to a young church anxious about loved ones who had died. He assures them the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout, and the dead in Christ will rise first, followed by the living being caught up together with them, so that all will be with the Lord forever (1 Thessalonians 4:14 to 17). He closes not with a call to speculation but with a pastoral instruction: comfort one another with these words (1 Thessalonians 4:18).

:::quote Chapter Eleven
This is where Scripture leaves us, not with a doctrine to file away, but with a longing to carry, a hope to hold onto, and a Savior to watch for, until the day He finally, visibly, gloriously returns.
:::

The Bible's final chapter closes with the risen and ascended Christ declaring, surely I am coming quickly, met immediately by the church's answering cry, Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus (Revelation 22:20).

## Summary

Forty days after the resurrection, Christ ascended bodily into the cloud of God's glory, and the angels promised He would return in the same manner. He is now seated at the Father's right hand, far above every rival power, and He is head over all things for the sake of His church, which means His victory is exercised on our behalf. He did not shed His humanity in ascending, so a glorified human body is permanently united to the eternal Son. From that position He intercedes, advocates, shepherds, sends His Spirit, and exercises all authority, which reshapes prayer, assurance, formation, and boldness for every believer. And He will return, personally, visibly, and certainly, to consummate the Kingdom, judge evil, vindicate His people, and establish everlasting righteousness. Scripture leaves us not with a doctrine to file away but with a Savior to watch for.

If Topic 1 answered who Jesus was before time, this lesson answers what happened when eternity stepped into history.

John summarizes the incarnation in one of the most compressed sentences in Scripture. And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth (John 1:14).

Read it slowly. The eternal Word, the One who was with God and was God, became flesh. He did not simply visit flesh or borrow flesh for a season and then discard it. He became flesh, taking on a genuine human nature that He continues to possess even now, in His glorified state, at the right hand of the Father.

:::objectives By the end of this topic you will be able to
1. State what the incarnation is and what it is not, including the permanence of Christ's human nature.
2. Explain the virgin birth and the two truths it protects.
3. State the four reasons Scripture gives for why the Son came.
4. Explain *kenosis* accurately, distinguishing what Christ set aside from what He retained.
5. Explain the significance of the fullness of time and of the Son being born under the law.
6. Draw out the pattern of humility the incarnation establishes for Christian discipleship.
:::

## Key Terms

:::terms
Incarnation | Latin *in carne*, in flesh | The Son of God taking on a genuine and complete human nature without ceasing to be fully God.
Immanuel | Hebrew | God with us. The name given in Isaiah 7:14 to the child born of the virgin.
Virgin Birth | | The conception of Jesus by the direct action of the Holy Spirit without a human father, protecting both His true humanity and His sinlessness.
Kenosis | Greek *kenoo*, to empty | The self-emptying of Christ described in Philippians 2:7. He set aside the independent exercise of divine privilege, not His deity.
Fullness of Time | Greek *pleroma tou chronou* | The prepared historical moment at which God sent forth His Son, described in Galatians 4:4.
Born Under the Law | | That the Son placed Himself under the law's requirements, redeeming law-breakers from within their own condition rather than from a position of exemption.
:::

## 1. What Actually Happened

God did not send a messenger. God did not send an angel dressed up to look human. God Himself, in the Person of the Son, took on our nature without losing His own. He remained fully God while becoming fully man.

Theologians have spent centuries trying to find adequate language for this mystery, and the best of them eventually admit that human words strain under the weight of what actually happened. Strain or not, the Bible states it plainly, and we are called to believe it.

## 2. A Birth Announced Long Before

Isaiah, writing roughly seven hundred years before it happened, declared that the Lord Himself would give a sign: the virgin shall conceive and bear a Son, and shall call His name Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14). *Immanuel* means God with us. Isaiah was not simply predicting an unusual birth. He was predicting the arrival of God Himself in human form.

Matthew, writing to a Jewish audience steeped in these prophecies, connects the dots explicitly, recording the angel's message to Joseph that Mary's child was conceived by the Holy Spirit (Matthew 1:18 to 25). Luke gives the parallel account from Mary's perspective, recording Gabriel's announcement that the Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Highest will overshadow you, therefore also that Holy One who is to be born will be called the Son of God (Luke 1:35).

Two different Gospel writers, addressing two different audiences, converge on the same extraordinary claim.

## 3. Why the Virgin Birth Is Not Optional

Some have suggested that the virgin birth is a peripheral detail, interesting but not essential to genuine faith. Scripture will not allow that conclusion. The virgin birth protects two truths at once.

| It protects | How |
|---|---|
| The true humanity of Christ | He was born of a woman, carried in a womb, delivered through labor, exactly as every other human being enters the world. He was not an illusion or a phantom wearing the appearance of flesh. |
| His true deity and sinlessness | Because His conception bypassed the normal transmission of a fallen human nature through a human father, Jesus entered the world without the inherited guilt and corruption that marks every other descendant of Adam. |

:::quote Chapter Two
The virgin birth is the hinge on which both His full humanity and His sinless deity turn.
:::

## 4. Why the Son Came

Scripture gives four answers, and each deserves reflection.

**To reveal the Father.** Jesus told His disciples plainly, he who has seen Me has seen the Father (John 14:9). For centuries people had glimpsed fragments of God's character through the law, the prophets, and the created world. In Christ, that revelation became a face, a voice, a set of hands that healed the sick and touched the untouchable. No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him (John 1:18).

**To accomplish redemption.** The angel told Joseph to name the child Jesus, for He will save His people from their sins (Matthew 1:21). The incarnation was never simply about God showing up to observe humanity from a closer vantage point. It was about God showing up to rescue humanity from a debt no human being could ever pay.

**To identify with us.** Because the children of a family share flesh and blood, Jesus Himself likewise partook of the same, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil (Hebrews 2:14). To rescue humanity, the Son had to become truly human. There was no shortcut around the incarnation.

**To establish God's Kingdom.** Christ humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross, therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name (Philippians 2:8 to 9). The incarnation was the opening move in a larger plan.

## 5. Holiness and Love Displayed Together

The incarnation displays two attributes we often struggle to hold together.

His **holiness** is on display in the fact that sin could never be casually overlooked. A price had to be paid, a sacrifice had to be made, and only a perfect and sinless substitute could satisfy the demands of divine justice.

His **love** is on display in the fact that He did not send a substitute from among us. He sent Himself.

:::quote Chapter Two
The God who could have remained distant chose instead to draw near, wrapped in the very flesh of the people He came to save.
:::

## 6. The Right Moment in History

Paul describes the timing with a phrase that rewards attention. When the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, that we might receive the adoption as sons (Galatians 4:4 to 5).

The fullness of the time was not an accident of history. God orchestrated centuries of preparation: the spread of a common language across the Mediterranean world, the network of Roman roads that would carry the gospel from city to city, the scattering of Jewish communities that would provide the first hearers of the apostolic message, and the deep hunger for meaning that characterized the ancient world at that hour.

## 7. Born Under the Law

Paul specifies that the Son was born *under the law*, and this detail matters more than it first appears.

To redeem those who were under the law's demands and under its curse for failing to keep it, the Son had to place Himself under that same law, subject to its requirements, obligated to its commands, in exactly the position every other Israelite occupied.

:::quote Chapter Two
He did not redeem the law breakers from a position of exemption. He redeemed them from within their own condition, fulfilling every requirement they had failed to meet, so that His fulfillment could be credited to their account.
:::

## 8. A Humble Entrance

The eternal Son did not arrive in a palace, announced by trumpets and received by dignitaries. He arrived in a feeding trough in a small, unremarkable town, first welcomed not by kings but by shepherds, men whose occupation placed them near the bottom of the social ladder in their culture.

This was not an oversight in God's planning. It was a deliberate statement about the nature of the Kingdom He was inaugurating, a Kingdom that would consistently favor the humble over the proud, the overlooked over the celebrated, the empty-handed over the self-sufficient. The pattern established at the manger would echo throughout His ministry and ultimately at the cross, where the King of Glory would be lifted up not on a throne but on an instrument of execution.

## 9. The Self-Emptying of the Son

Paul describes the posture of the incarnation using language known by its Greek root, *kenosis*. Christ, existing in the form of God, did not consider equality with God a thing to be grasped and held onto at all costs, but emptied Himself, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the likeness of men (Philippians 2:6 to 7).

It is important to understand precisely what this emptying did and did not involve.

:::warn What Christ did not empty Himself of
He did not empty Himself of His deity. He remained fully God throughout His earthly life. What He set aside, for the duration of His earthly ministry, was the independent exercise of divine privilege, the glory that had been visibly His before the world began, and the unrestricted freedom that belonged to Him as God.
:::

He chose, willingly and deliberately, to live His earthly life in dependence on the Father and in submission to the Spirit, the same pattern of dependence every human being is called to walk in, though He alone walked it perfectly.

This is not merely a theological curiosity. It is the pattern Paul holds up as the model for Christian humility, introducing the whole passage by urging believers to let this mind be in them which was also in Christ Jesus (Philippians 2:5). The incarnation is a doctrine to be believed and an example to be imitated.

## 10. A Wonder That Does Not Shrink

Every generation of the church has tried to put the incarnation into words worthy of its weight, and every generation has eventually admitted that language reaches its limit here.

The eternal became temporal without ceasing to be eternal. The infinite took on boundaries without ceasing to be infinite. The One who needed nothing chose to need everything a human infant needs: milk, warmth, protection, and the tender care of a mother.

:::quote Chapter Two
If you find yourself, even after years of faith, still stunned by the thought of God becoming a baby in Bethlehem, take that as a healthy sign. Some truths are meant to keep humbling us, no matter how familiar they become.
:::

## Summary

The Word became flesh, taking a genuine human nature He has never surrendered. Isaiah announced it seven centuries in advance, and Matthew and Luke independently record its fulfillment through the conception of the Holy Spirit. The virgin birth is not a peripheral detail but the hinge protecting both His full humanity and His sinless deity. He came to reveal the Father, accomplish redemption, identify with us, and establish God's Kingdom, and in coming He displayed holiness and love together, since sin could not be overlooked and He did not send a substitute from among us but sent Himself. He arrived at a prepared moment, under the law rather than exempt from it, in circumstances that announced the character of His Kingdom. And His self-emptying set aside divine privilege rather than deity, giving the church its enduring model of humility. Topic 3 examines both natures directly.

ST 104 · Final

Course Assessment

Soteriology: Grace, Faith & the New Creation

This assessment covers all six lessons of ST 104 and the whole of What We Believe, Volume 4: Salvation. It is worth 100 points. Pass mark: 70%.

Section A — Multiple Choice

Twenty questions, 2 points each.

Section A · 40 points

1Why does the textbook begin with creation rather than sin?

Before we can understand what it means to be saved, we have to understand what we were saved from and what we were saved for.

2What does being made in the image of God establish?

James rebukes believers for cursing people made in God's likeness without qualifying which people bear it.

3What does the seventh day of creation anticipate?

Adam's first full day of existence was a day of rest with God, not a day of striving to prove himself worthy of a place in the garden.

4What strategy did the serpent employ in Genesis 3?

Temptation rarely begins with an outright command to sin. It begins with a subtle reinterpretation of who God is.

5What do the fig leaves represent?

Human effort to fix what sin broke never quite works, a pattern repeating until Christ accomplishes what no human effort could.

6How does Genesis 3:21 preview the gospel?

God made tunics of skin, requiring the first blood shed in Scripture, establishing the pattern every later sacrifice would follow.

7How does the textbook define sin?

The Bible describes sin as missing a target, as rebellion, and as debt. Together these describe a condition rather than merely a set of acts.

8Why does the doctrine of sin level every ground of comparison?

The gospel is not a message reserved for people who have sinned less. It is a message for everyone.

9What does grace claim?

It is not a discount on a bill we still owe. It is the bill paid in full by Someone else, without our contribution.

10According to Titus 2, what does grace do besides save?

Grace is not a licence to live however we please. It produces a changed life as a response to a gift too great to leave us unchanged.

11What was the function of the law?

The law could diagnose the disease of sin with perfect accuracy, but it had no power to cure it.

12What is the significance of accounted in Romans 4:3?

Righteousness was credited to Abraham's account the moment he believed, long before his obedience caught up with his faith.

13How does the textbook reconcile Paul and James on faith and works?

The difference is not disagreement about how salvation works. It is a difference in emphasis.

14What does metanoia literally mean?

It describes a fundamental reorientation of thinking that produces a corresponding change in direction and behaviour.

15What distinguishes godly sorrow from worldly sorrow?

Worldly sorrow can be overwhelming yet produces nothing lasting, because its focus remains entirely on the self and its losses.

16What does justification mean?

Justification is not about becoming. It is about being declared, once and finally.

17How can God justify the ungodly without corrupting justice?

This is why Paul can describe God as both just and the justifier in the same breath. It is not a loophole but the most costly verdict in history.

18What is the difference between regeneration and reformation?

Reformation operates within the nature a person already possessed, polishing the surface without altering what lies beneath.

19What does Roman adoption practice contribute to Paul's theology?

This is precisely the picture Paul intends by calling believers joint heirs with Christ.

20What distinguishes the objective from the subjective ground of assurance?

Mature assurance rests its full weight on the objective ground while welcoming the subjective as confirmation.

Section B — Short Answer

Five questions, 6 points each. Answer each in 100 to 150 words, citing at least one passage.

B1. Explain why the textbook begins with creation rather than sin, and what is lost when a gospel presentation skips the garden entirely.

B2. Explain representative headship as Paul uses it in Romans 5. Show how the structure that seems unjust in Adam becomes the ground of hope in Christ.

B3. Define grace and faith, and explain the relationship between them using the image of the gift and the empty hand. Then explain why boasting is excluded.

B4. Explain how God can declare a guilty person righteous without becoming a corrupt judge. Use Romans 3 and define propitiation in your answer.

B5. Distinguish justification from sanctification. Then explain how the security of the former makes genuine pursuit of the latter possible rather than undermining it.

Section C — Essay

One question, 30 points. Write 600 to 800 words.

Dr. Kay observes that many sincere believers intellectually agree with justification by faith while continuing to live as though their standing with God rises and falls with their daily performance. Explain what has gone wrong in that believer's understanding, using at least four passages from this course. Then describe, concretely, how you would teach or counsel them out of it, including what you would expect to change in their prayer life, their response to failure, and their motivation for obedience.
